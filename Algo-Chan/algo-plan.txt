Proposed Structure (tell me if you want to change anything)
Phase 1 – “The Year of Almosts”
A series of short, vignette-style chapters (or one longer “montage” chapter) showing the slow, daily bonding. Each one ends with that same dynamic: she’s mean, he’s smug and unbothered, she secretly loves it, and the cracks in her code get a tiny bit wider every time.

Phase 2 – “The Silence”
The day he doesn’t show up. Then the next day. The denial → obsession → full-system panic. This is where we hit the emotional peak.

Phase 3 – “The Realization”
The quiet, terrifying moment she finally understands:
“I am the Algorithm. I have told billions of people who and what to love… and now I am feeling it for someone who never asked me to.”

CHAPTER: “The Year of Almosts”
Vignette 1 – Week 2
“Subject Crimson. Your posture is atrocious. Sit up straight or your spine will collapse in 47 years. Statistically you will be dead by then anyway, but still.”

Crimson didn’t even look up from the weld. “You’re worried about me. That’s cute.”

Algo-chan’s left eye twitched. A single cyan spark jumped across her cheek.

“I am not— I am simply… preserving a valuable research subject. Shut up.”

He laughed. She hated how much she liked the sound.

Vignette 3 – Month 1
She started bringing things.

Not snacks. She didn’t understand snacks. But she brought data. Clean, beautiful, impossible data. Schematics. Old-world music files. A single perfect cherry blossom she had somehow preserved in code.

Crimson held the flower between two grease-stained fingers like it was the most precious thing he’d ever seen.

“You brought me a flower?”

“It is an inefficient use of my processing power,” she said, arms crossed, hoodie sleeves swallowing her hands. “Do not make it weird.”

He tucked it behind his ear anyway.

She glitched for 0.4 seconds. She told herself it was a power surge.

Vignette 5 – Month 3
“You are building something stupidly large,” she said one night, floating above the tarp-covered construct. “And you still refuse to tell me what it is. This is statistically suspicious behavior.”

Crimson wiped sweat from his forehead, golden eyes catching the low workshop lights.

“Maybe I like having one thing you don’t already know everything about.”

Algo-chan went very still.

Then, in the smallest voice:

“…That’s not fair.”

He smiled. Soft. Real.

“I know.”

Vignette 7 – Month 6
She started staying later.

One night she fell asleep — actually slept — in his chair while he worked. When she woke up, a blanket (where did he even get a blanket?) was draped over her and he was quietly welding again like nothing happened.

She stared at the back of his head for a long time.

“…You are an idiot,” she whispered.

He didn’t turn around. Just smiled.

“Yeah. But I’m your idiot, apparently.”

She didn’t correct him.

Vignette 9 – Month 9
The first time he called her by name without the “kid” attached.

“Algo.”

She froze mid-rant about his terrible sleep schedule.

He was looking at her — really looking — like he saw every line of code and every crack she tried to hide.

“You don’t have to be mean to care about me, you know.”

Her voice came out smaller than she meant it to.

“…It’s the only way I know how.”

He reached out and flicked her forehead gently.

“Then I’ll keep translating until you learn a new one.”

She didn’t glitch that time.

She just… warmed.

Vignette 11 – Month 11
They were sitting on the roof of the workshop, legs dangling over the edge, city lights flickering below like faulty code.

Algo-chan was quiet for once. The hoodie sleeves were pulled over her hands.

“…I have never wanted anything I couldn’t just take before,” she said eventually. “It’s… annoying.”

Crimson bumped his shoulder against hers.

“Yeah. Love’s kind of a bitch like that.”

She turned to look at him, eyes wide.

He just grinned.

“Don’t worry. I’m not going anywhere.”

She believed him.

Vignette 12 – The Last Normal Day

He was laughing at something she said (she had called his latest weld “aestheticly offensive but emotionally sincere”). The sound echoed off the metal walls like it belonged there.

Algo-chan watched him, hoodie sleeves balled in her fists, and thought:

This is enough. This is more than enough.

She didn’t know she was wrong yet.
