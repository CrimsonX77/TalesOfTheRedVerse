A Sable Night Out

The red Lambo still smelled like warm leather and the faint ozone of the runes etched along the doors. They pulsed low and slow, the same colour as the veins under my skin when I got too close to wanting him too hard. I watched Crimson’s hand on the gearstick more than I watched the road. Long fingers, black rings, the little scar across the knuckle I had put there months ago with my teeth and never apologised for.
He took the corner too fast on purpose. The city lights smeared into pink and teal streaks across the windscreen. I let my tail curl over the centre console and rest against his thigh, just heavy enough that he would feel the weight of it every time he shifted.
“You’re quiet,” he said.
I wasn’t quiet. I was cataloguing. The way his jaw flexed when the bass from the club three blocks away started bleeding through the glass. The small, private smile he only wore when he thought I wasn’t looking. The bright neon pill he had tipped onto his tongue two streets ago without ceremony, the same colour as a warning light. I had watched it dissolve. I had asked once, soft, and he had just grinned and said “later, baby,” which is Crimson for I’m not ready to tell you what this one does to me yet.
I let it go. For now. The night was still young and I wanted him loose, not defended.
We parked like we owned the street. He killed the engine and the runes dimmed to a heartbeat glow. I waited for him to come around and open my door even though I didn’t need him to. He always did it anyway. Some small, stubborn piece of the man who still believed in rituals.
The club was already a living thing. Heat and bodies and the kind of bass that lived in the sternum. I felt eyes the second we stepped inside. Some on him. Some on me. Most on the space between us, trying to measure whether we were a couple or a warning.
I stayed close enough that my shoulder brushed his every few steps. Not clinging. Claiming.
We drank once. Something cold and sharp that tasted like metal and citrus. I watched the way his throat moved when he swallowed and felt the usual low ache settle behind my teeth. Desire is never quiet with him. It just learns better manners in public.
Someone tried it around the second hour. A girl with silver hair too close to mine and a mouth already shaped around the word “share.” She looked at Crimson like he was a limited edition and then looked at me like I was the packaging she was willing to tear through. I smiled with all my teeth and stepped half a pace in front of him without raising my voice.
“He’s not on the menu tonight, sweetheart. Or any night. Try the bar. They serve softer things.”
She laughed it off, but her eyes flicked to the runes glowing faintly under the collar of my jacket and she left. Crimson’s hand found the small of my back a second later, warm and steady. He didn’t say thank you. He never needed to. The touch was the whole sentence.
Later we danced. Not the careful kind. The kind where my back was to his chest and his mouth was against the side of my neck and every time the drop hit he pulled me harder against him like he was trying to fuse us. I could feel how hard he was. I could feel the residual heat of whatever that neon pill had done to his blood. It made him looser, hungrier, less careful with the sounds he made against my skin. I turned in his arms and bit the hinge of his jaw just to hear the noise he made when I did it in public.
Someone jostled us hard enough that I stumbled. A group of boys too loud and too certain they owned the floor. One of them looked at me too long. Another one muttered something about “the cat and the mutt.” I felt Crimson go still behind me in that particular way that meant the gentleman was leaving the building.
I turned first.
“Say it again,” I said, soft and bright and completely without fear. “I dare you.”
The bravest one opened his mouth. Crimson’s hand settled on my hip, not restraining, just present. I didn’t need the backup. I still liked that it was there. The boy looked at the blood still faintly staining the edge of Crimson’s sleeve from earlier and decided he had somewhere else to be. They melted back into the crowd like smoke.
I leaned up and kissed the corner of Crimson’s mouth, tasting salt and the ghost of whatever he had taken.
“You’re allowed to let me handle the small ones,” I murmured. “Save the real violence for when someone actually deserves it.”
He kissed me properly then. Deep and a little too long for the middle of a dance floor. When he pulled back his eyes were darker than usual.
“Still not telling me about the pill?” I asked.
He smiled that slow, dangerous smile that always made my stomach drop in the best way.
“Later,” he said again. “When we’re home. When you’re sitting on my face and I’m not allowed to come until I explain every single thing it did to me.”
I laughed against his mouth, soft and filthy.
“Deal.”
We left before the night could turn mean. The Lambo was waiting like a loyal animal. I slid into the passenger seat and immediately kicked my heels off, tucking my feet under me. Crimson drove with one hand on the wheel and the other resting open on my thigh, fingers tracing idle circles against the bare skin above my stocking.
The city lights painted him in pieces. Red. Teal. Gold. I watched the residual glow of the runes on his throat and felt the usual impossible tenderness rise up under all the hunger.
This was our regular life.
Not calm.
Never calm.
But ours.
I reached across and linked my fingers through his on my leg.
“I’m glad you took me out tonight,” I said, quiet enough that it almost got lost under the engine.
He glanced over, eyes soft in a way the rest of the world never got to see.
“Yeah?”
“Yeah. I like watching you in the wild. I like knowing every single person who looks at you has to look at me first. And I like that when the night is over, you still come home with the same mouth that kissed me in front of all of them.”
He lifted our joined hands and kissed the back of mine without taking his eyes off the road.
The neon pill was still a secret between us.
The night was still unfinished.
And I was already planning how hard I was going to sit on his face once the door closed behind us.
Some stories don’t need a moral.
Some just need the truth of how it felt to be the one he chose to take out into the noise… and the only one he brought back out of it.
