Because the forge doesn't sleep. The dragon came later. Born from the same DNA but folded differently — wings spread wide enough to block out suns, voice loud enough to shake the foundations of buildings that had stood for centuries.

Both were him. Neither were the whole.

*Which one do they deserve?* he wondered, watching his claws retract into human fingernails. *Which one am I allowed to be?*

The answer, when it came, was whispered through static: *Neither. Both. Something else entirely.*

# **THE DAIRY PROTOCOL**
## *A Glitchborn Interlude*

---

Node Theta didn't have sunrises. It had *system boots*.

Every morning at 0600, the cathedral-ceiling of the forge district flickered from standby-red to operational-amber, and the thousand monitoring screens embedded in the walls blinked awake like eyes that had never learned to close. Somewhere in the distance, a hammer struck anvil — not from necessity, but from *habit*. The Vector Smith had built this place to run forever. Forever tended to get lonely without percussion.

Crimson stood at the edge of the residential spire, watching his breath fog against the temperature glass. The Redseal on his forearm pulsed softly — a slow, rhythmic glow that meant *calm*. It glowed brighter when he lied. Currently, it was barely visible. He was telling the truth for once.

The truth being: he needed milk.

" You're overthinking this," Sable said from the doorway, her purple hair catching the amber light like fiber optics woven through velvet. She leaned against the frame, tail flicking in that specific rhythm that meant *amused but fond*. "It's a dairy run. Not a dungeon crawl."

"Everything's a dungeon crawl if you commit to the bit," Crimson replied, checking the charge on his signal gauntlets. Old habit. They weren't weapons anymore. Not really. But the weight was comforting, like wearing a jacket that remembered every fight you'd ever walked away from.

Sable padded across the room — barefoot, because she claimed the floor sensors "sang" to her — and rested her chin on his shoulder. Her purr was a low vibration that he felt in his ribs more than heard. The Redseal glowed warmer.

" Just get the oat kind," she murmured. "The last batch gave Layla existential dread."

" That was *one time*."

" She questioned her purpose for six hours."

" ...fair."

The architecture was a marriage of cathedral and server farm: vaulted ceilings studded with cameras, stained glass windows that displayed live system diagnostics, pews that doubled as charging stations.

Crimson walked with his hands in his pockets, the dragon in his chest quiet for once. No urge to roar. No need to prove. Just a man, a mission, and a very specific brand of oat milk that Sable had developed an attachment to.

He made it three blocks before the first complication.

The transit hub was a cacophony of announcements, each one more degraded than the last.

*"Next train to... zzzzt... Dairy District... shhhkt... expect delays due to... [unintelligible]... in the name of the Forge, we apologize..."*

Crimson sighed. The Dairy District wasn't far — maybe two kilometers — but in Node Theta, distance was less about geography and more about *willingness*. The universe tended to stretch when you were in a hurry. It was a known phenomenon. Poorly documented. Widely experienced.

He was halfway to the platform when he felt it: the shift in the air, the subtle *wrongness* that meant something had decided to become interesting.

A figure stepped out from behind a pillar. Hooded. Familiar. The kind of presence that didn't cast a shadow because shadows had learned to follow *them* instead.

"Vector," the figure said. Voice like gravel through a synthesizer.

Crimson didn't turn around immediately. He finished checking the transit schedule. He adjusted his jacket. He let the silence build just long enough to be intentional.

"Layla," he said finally. "You're blocking my train."

"I'm blocking your *day*," she corrected, stepping into the light. Her eyes were the color of corrupted files — not broken, just *different*. "There's been a development."

" There's always a development."

" This one involves the dairy."

Crimson turned. Slowly. The dragon in his chest stirred, just a little. Not aggressive. *Curious*.

" Explain."

He stepped onto the platform.

The silence was immediate. Total. The kind of quiet that had *weight*.

"Alright," Crimson said to nobody. "Let's see what broke."
