**Chapter 2: Run or Burn**

The deck beneath them was an unsteady mess of sparks, ruptured plating, and emergency lights casting everything in deep crimson. The ship groaned—a dying beast bleeding atmosphere into the void. 

Crimson’s grip was firm but careful around Navi’s wrist as he pulled her forward—not yanking, not rough, but **precise**. Because efficiency meant more than just broken bones. Efficiency meant *getting her out alive.*

They sprinted through the corridor, dodging twisted metal and sparking conduits. The air reeked of burning insulation and ozone, thick smoke rolling in from deeper sections of the ship. Every few seconds, the hull shuddered—another impact, another section of their world breaking apart.

The MechaNavi bay was ahead—**but so were the enemy.**

Navi barely skidded to a stop, Crimson instinctively **shielding her with his body** as a squad of black-armored operatives spilled into the corridor ahead. **Strike team. Well-trained. Well-armed. Not here for prisoners.**

Crimson didn’t hesitate. **Two shots, two kills.** Dead center. 

But there were more.

“**Tell me you’ve got a plan.**”

Navi’s eyes darted to a supply crate marked ‘PRESSURIZED PROPULSION MODULES’—**small thruster packs used for EVA maneuvering.**

A grin tugged at her lips. “**I always have a plan. You’re just never gonna like it.**”

Before Crimson could protest, she bolted forward, **grabbing a thruster pack, slamming the ignition, and hurling it down the corridor.** 

**BOOM.** The force of the explosion sent the operatives reeling, bodies crashing into the walls as fire licked across the metal.

“**RUN!**” Navi shouted, already dragging Crimson forward.

Gunfire erupted behind them. Bullets ricocheted off the walls, but Crimson was already turning, **firing without breaking stride**—three precise shots, dropping two more enemies before they made the hangar doors. 

Navi slammed her fist against the console. “**OPEN, DAMN IT!**”

The security system hesitated. Crimson swore. “**We’re locked out! The ship’s AI is failing!**”

Behind them, the strike team regrouped. Heavy boots pounded against the deck. **Too close.**

Navi’s gaze flicked up—an exposed maintenance hatch, leading directly into the hangar’s upper scaffolding. 

She turned to Crimson, smirking despite the chaos. “**Time for Plan B.**”

His eyes narrowed. “**You’re gonna make me jump, aren’t you?**”

“**You think I’d leave you behind?**” she shot back, already reaching for the first rung.

Crimson hesitated for just a fraction of a second, watching her climb—**not because he doubted the plan, but because in that moment, between the fire and the bullets, she was everything.**

And then he moved. 

With a burst of motion, **he leapt up, catching the railing effortlessly and hauling himself after her.** Below, another hail of bullets tore through the space where they had stood moments before. 

The strike team wasn’t stopping. **Neither were they.**

Above, the MechaNavi loomed, its massive frame towering over the chaos, waiting.

If they could just reach it—**they had a fighting chance.**
