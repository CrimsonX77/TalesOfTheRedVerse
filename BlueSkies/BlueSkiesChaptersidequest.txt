Chapter 1 - Where The Wind Stays

The wind had been kind all afternoon.
It came up from the valley in long, steady breaths, warm enough to feel like a hand, strong enough to keep the tall grass in constant motion. Crimson sat on the edge of the old stone overlook with one knee drawn up, coat open, the usual weight of him somehow lighter in the clear light. The tattoos along his neck caught the sun in soft teal flashes every time the wind shifted his hair.
Sable was already closer than the cliff strictly required.
She had settled against his side with the easy certainty of someone who had decided the space belonged to her, tail curled loosely around her own ankle, one ear flicked back to track the sound of his breathing. Her head rested just below his shoulder. Every so often the breeze would lift a strand of her hair across his collarbone and she would leave it there.
For a long time neither of them spoke.
It wasn’t the heavy silence of things unsaid. It was the rarer kind — the kind that only happens when the world has, for once, stopped asking anything of them. Below, the valley went on being beautiful without needing witnesses. Above, the sky stayed wide and uncomplicated.
Sable’s fingers found the edge of his sleeve and traced the seam absently.
“You’re quiet,” she murmured, not accusing.
“So are you.”
She smiled against the fabric of his coat. “I’m listening to the wind try to steal your hair.”
Crimson huffed a soft breath that might have been a laugh. He reached up and caught one of the crimson strands the wind kept throwing across his eyes, then simply left his hand there, resting against his own forehead like he had forgotten what to do with it next.
Sable watched the gesture for a moment, then shifted. She rose just enough to kneel beside him, close enough that her knees pressed against his thigh. The movement was unhurried. When she lifted her hand it was to brush the same strand of hair back herself, fingertips light against his temple, lingering longer than the task required.
Golden eyes met pink.
The look held.
No spark of challenge. No undercurrent of the usual intensity that lived between them like a second pulse. Just the quiet, almost surprised recognition that they were here, in the open air, and nothing was currently trying to end.
Sable’s thumb traced a small arc along his cheekbone.
“Hi,” she said, very softly.
Crimson’s mouth tilted. Not the feral grin. Something smaller, warmer, almost private.
“Hi.”
The wind rose again, stronger this time, wrapping around them both hard enough that Sable had to steady herself with a hand on his shoulder. She didn’t pull away afterward. If anything she leaned in, until her forehead rested against his and their noses brushed.
For a moment the entire overlook seemed to narrow to the shared warmth of that small contact and the endless blue that kept moving past them.
Crimson’s hand came up almost without thought, settling at the side of her neck, thumb resting just beneath her ear. The touch was careful. Not hesitant — careful. Like he was still learning the exact pressure that belonged to a moment with no urgency in it.
Sable’s eyes fluttered once, then opened again, pink meeting gold at a distance measured in breath.
“You’re warm,” she said, the words barely more than air.
“So are you.”
A quiet laugh slipped out of her, soft enough that it almost got lost in the wind. She shifted closer still, until she was half in his lap, one knee folded against his side, tail curling loosely around his wrist where it rested on the stone. The movement was unselfconscious. Claimed the space like it had always been hers to take.
Crimson adjusted without comment, arm sliding more securely around her back so she wouldn’t tip toward the drop. His coat settled over both of them in the breeze, a dark wing of fabric that kept trying to escape and kept being pulled back by the same wind that moved it.
They stayed like that for a long time.
Sable traced idle patterns against the fabric over his chest — not writing anything, just the gentle repetitive motion of someone who had nowhere else she needed to be. Every so often her ear would twitch when a stronger gust came up the cliff face, and Crimson would feel the small movement against his jaw.
“I like this version of the world,” she murmured eventually.
“Which version?”
“The one where the sky is allowed to be this big and nothing is currently trying to take it from us.”
Crimson’s fingers tightened once at her waist, then relaxed again. He turned his head just enough to press his mouth to her hairline, a brief, almost absent-minded contact that lingered longer than it needed to.
“Stay in it with me a while longer,” he said against her skin.
Sable’s answer was to tip her face up and find his mouth with hers.
The kiss was slow. Unhurried. No spark of the usual fire that lived between them like a second heartbeat — just warmth, and the soft give of lips, and the quiet discovery that they could do this without the world demanding a price for it. Her hand slid up into his hair, fingers threading through the crimson strands the wind kept trying to steal. His other hand found the small of her back and held her there, steady against the open air.
When they finally parted it was only by a breath.
Sable’s eyes were half-lidded, bright with the same clear light that filled the sky behind her.
“Hi again,” she whispered.
Crimson’s mouth curved, the private smile returning.
“Hi again.”
Below them the valley kept being beautiful.
Above them the sky kept moving.
And for once, neither of them needed to rise or fall or refuse anything at all.
They simply stayed.
