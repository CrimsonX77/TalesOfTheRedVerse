**Chapter 4: The Last Transmission**

The cockpit was shaking apart. Red warning lights flashed across every screen. **Everything was on fire.**

Outside the viewport, the shipyards burned. Pieces of debris—**the remains of their fleet**—floated through the void, illuminated only by muzzle flashes and the raging inferno consuming the capital ships. **And at the center of it all, The Admiral’s command bridge was burning.**

Crimson was watching it, eyes locked on the spiraling wreckage as the last known coordinates of The Admiral’s ship **blipped out of existence.**

"**Come on… come on, signal! Give me something!**" he snarled, hammering at the console.

Navi’s hands were gripping the controls, knuckles white, breathing sharp. **She wasn’t looking.** She refused to look. **If she didn’t see it, it wasn’t real.**

Then, a burst of static. **A voice.**

"—Navi. Listen to me. You have to—" **CRACK.** More static. **Fire washing over the bridge.** A silhouette barely visible through the burning glass. **The Admiral.**

"Grandfather—?!" Navi’s voice broke.

Another impact. The ship tilted. The transmission warped into screaming alarms. 

Then, **silence.**

Navi’s breath hitched. Crimson didn’t say a word. **There was nothing left to say.**

A new explosion ripped through the hull behind them. The MechaNavi shook, warning sirens flaring—

"**NAV! JUMP!**"

The force of the final detonation propelled them **into the abyss.**

Fire. Metal. Silence.

**F A D E  T O  B L A C K.**

---

### **Chapter 5: Three Days Later**

The desert heat was a **living thing.** It clung to the air, thick and dry, wrapping around them like a heavy blanket. The sound of distant windchimes rattled lazily in the warm breeze.

Navi sat slumped in a wooden chair on the rooftop of a small, run-down **borderworld cantina**, staring at the empty glass on the table in front of her. Her flight suit was still singed in places, boots caked with dust. She hadn’t spoken much in days.

Crimson leaned against the railing, arms crossed, watching the twin suns dip toward the horizon. His own uniform was faded, torn, stitched back together with whatever local scraps he could find. **He looked just as wrecked as she did.**

For a long time, neither of them spoke.

Then, finally, Navi exhaled. **A slow, deliberate breath.**

"He’s dead, isn’t he?"

Crimson didn’t answer right away. Instead, he pulled a battered comm device from his belt, flipped it over in his hand. The last thing The Admiral ever said to them was still stored in its fractured memory.

"I don’t know," he admitted finally. "But if he’s alive… he won’t be the same."

Navi laughed—**a hollow, empty sound.**

"Yeah. Guess that makes two of us."

---

**Chapter 6: The Weight of Silence**

The cantina smelled like dust and stale liquor, the kind of place where names didn’t matter and no one asked questions. The kind of place where ghosts went to drink.

Crimson rolled the edge of his glass between his fingers, watching the cheap amber liquid swirl but never drinking. The alcohol wasn’t for him—it was for show, for blending in, for the illusion that sitting here meant he had somewhere to be.

Navi, on the other hand, had long since abandoned the pretense. Her glass sat untouched beside a half-empty bottle, her gaze locked on something distant—something neither of them could see. **The space between breaths. The silence between screams.**

Three days, and still no words that mattered.

Crimson finally broke the quiet. "We can’t stay here forever."

Navi blinked slowly, like it took effort to pull herself out of the fog. "Not in a rush to get shot in the back?"

"Depends. You planning to keep making enemies at every stop?"

She smirked—barely. "That’s kind of my thing."

Crimson exhaled, pushing his glass aside. "We need a plan."

She scoffed, leaning back in her chair. "What’s the plan, Crimson? Steal another ship? Get another fleet blown to hell? Pretend we aren’t two dead people walking?"

His jaw tightened. "That’s not what he would’ve wanted."

Navi’s smirk vanished. Her fingers drummed against the table once. Twice. Then stopped.

"Don’t." Her voice was quieter now, but sharper. **Like a wound freshly stitched, fragile but holding.**

Crimson didn’t press. Not now.

Instead, he turned his attention to the cantina’s holo-screen, where a news report scrolled across the bar’s murky glow.

**Fleet Headquarters Destroyed in Unprovoked Attack**

A distorted image of The Admiral’s ship—**or what was left of it.**

Navi didn’t look. **She didn’t need to.**

Crimson’s grip tightened on the table. "They’re lying."

Navi let out a bitter laugh. "Welcome to the universe."

A long silence stretched between them before she finally pushed her chair back, standing. "Come on. We both know how this ends."

Crimson eyed her warily. "Do we?"

She grabbed her jacket off the back of her chair, slinging it over her shoulders. "Yeah. We find out who pulled the trigger. And we send them to hell."

Crimson watched her for a moment before standing too, adjusting the strap of his sidearm. **Something in his chest settled. Something in his chest burned.**

"Alright then," he said. "Let’s get to work."

---
**Chapter 7: Blood in the Dust**

The cantina was alive with the usual sounds—glasses clinking, low murmurs of hushed deals, the occasional burst of laughter from a group too deep into their drinks. A place where killers, smugglers, and survivors all gathered under one unspoken rule: **Don’t start something you can’t finish.**

Crimson wasn’t paying attention to any of it. His mind was on **what came next.** How they moved forward. How they didn’t drown in grief.

Then he heard it. **Laughter. Loud, ugly, drunk.**

"Ha! I tell ya, the way that command ship folded like a cheap tin can? Priceless! Bunch of high and mighty fleet bastards—screamin’ like rats in a fire."

The world around them **slowed.**

Navi **stopped walking.**

Crimson barely had time to react before she was already moving. **Silent. Deadly. A storm on the verge of breaking.**

"Nav—"

Too late.

The drunk loudmouth barely had time to blink before **Navi’s fist shattered his jaw.**

A chair scraped against the floor as he **crashed backward**, sending bottles flying, knocking over a table. The bar went **silent.**

Crimson exhaled. **Here we go.**

"You think that’s funny?" Navi growled, shaking the sting from her knuckles before grabbing the man **by the collar and dragging him up.**

He coughed—tried to form words, but his jaw was already swelling, teeth loose from the impact. His eyes darted around, looking for help—

**There was none.**

"I asked you a question." Navi slammed him against the bar counter this time, bottles rattling, glass shattering. **Her voice was low, controlled—but there was something in it. Something dangerous.**

"I-I didn’t mean—"

**CRACK.**

Another hit. Blood now. The man whimpered, weak hands pushing at her grip. **Panic setting in.**

Crimson let it go at first. **Let her have this.** Let her take something back from the void that had swallowed everything else.

But then he saw it.

The way her fingers tightened. The way her breathing changed. **She wasn’t just angry.** She was breaking.

**And she was going too far.**

"Nav. That’s enough."

She didn’t move. Didn’t acknowledge him.

The man was barely upright now, blood dripping from his busted lip, breath coming in **sharp, terrified gasps.**

"Navi. Let go."

Her knuckles were white. The rage was still there—**the pain, the loss, the screaming void inside her.**

Crimson **grabbed her wrist.**

"I said, let go."

For half a second, **she resisted.**

Then she dropped him.

The man crumpled to the floor, gasping, scrambling back on his elbows like a wounded animal.

Crimson didn’t look at him. His eyes were on Navi. **She was still shaking.**

Then, the silence broke.

The drunk staggered to his feet, wiping blood from his face with the back of his sleeve. His eyes flicked toward a group in the corner. **Men who had been watching.**

**His people.**

"You—you don’t know who you’re messin’ with," he slurred, voice full of panic and pride. "You’re dead. Both of you."

The men in the corner stood up. **One by one.**

A dozen, maybe more. **Heavily armed.**

The bartender sighed and ducked behind the counter.

Crimson exhaled, rolling his shoulders. "Great. So much for low profile."

Navi wiped her mouth with the back of her hand, glancing at him. **Her eyes were clear now. Focused.**

"You complaining?"

Crimson smirked. "Not yet."

The first shot was fired.

Then **all hell broke loose.**

---
**Chapter 8: Surrounded**

Navi’s smile faded instantly. The air shifted—electric, charged with danger. Crimson tensed, feeling the atmosphere tighten around them like a noose.

"Well, well," drawled a rough voice from behind them, "you two sure made a mess of my boy."

They turned slowly. The cantina had gone silent, patrons quickly slipping out, leaving only the heavy thud of boots on the wooden floor. Men in stained black clothes, bearing scars and tattoos marking them as part of some ruthless gang, had surrounded them. Cold, used steel barrels pointed unwaveringly at Navi and Crimson's faces.

At their center stood a tall man, his face twisted into a malicious sneer. He was older, grizzled, with a thick, ugly scar that ran down his cheek. "Didn’t anyone tell you? This is my town."

Navi tilted her head, unfazed despite the danger. "Funny, I didn't see your name on it."

Crimson’s fingers twitched, instinctively calculating angles, exits, and potential cover. He exchanged a quick glance with Navi—a single look that spoke volumes. **Trust me.**

The gang leader chuckled darkly, signaling his men to step closer. "You’ve got spirit, girl. I like that. Shame you’re not gonna live long enough to use it."

"Don’t count on it," Crimson growled, his voice low and deadly.

The leader raised his gun, aiming it directly at Navi’s forehead. "Any last words?"

Navi smirked, the fire returning to her eyes. "Just two."

He hesitated, curious despite himself. "Oh?"

Her smirk widened. "**Lights out.**"

Crimson moved in perfect sync, drawing and firing before the words had fully left her lips. The single shot shattered the hanging light above them, plunging the cantina into sudden darkness.

Chaos erupted.

Gunfire, shouts, and the sound of breaking glass filled the air. Navi and Crimson were already moving—fluid, deadly, and utterly in sync. This wasn’t a fight. This was survival.

In the dark, they had the advantage. And they would not waste it.


**Chapter 9: The Impossible Ballet**

The world exploded into chaos. Bullets roared like thunder, tearing apart the cantina in a storm of splinters, glass, and shattered dreams. Time slowed, each heartbeat echoing like the drumbeat of war, a savage rhythm pounding in sync with their surging adrenaline.

Forty-five guns aimed at just two souls—Navi and Crimson. Odds stacked beyond impossible. Yet they stood back-to-back, untouched, in the eye of a storm of their own making.

It began.

Navi moved first—a whisper of lethal grace, sliding forward on her knees beneath a hailstorm of bullets. Her twin pistols danced, spitting lead in elegant arcs, each shot placed as if she'd rehearsed this moment a thousand times. She hadn't, but every bullet knew its mark.

Three enemies fell, their surprise frozen eternally on their faces. She rose without pause, twirling away from a shotgun blast as if it were a mere gust of wind. She spun toward Crimson, eyes locked, knowing without words what came next.

Crimson moved in brutal harmony, shotgun roaring like a beast unchained. A flicker of movement caught his eye—a sniper hidden on the roof. Too late to dodge, too impossible to evade. Yet he acted as if the outcome were certain.

His boot hit a discarded stop sign lying on the floor. It rose with the perfection of a dance partner, spinning upward at the exact angle to deflect the sniper’s bullet. Sparks showered like fireworks, the sign clattering aside as Crimson fired twice, the sniper tumbling lifelessly into the dirt.

They moved like dancers in a ballet of death, every step choreographed by a force greater than themselves. Navi flipped over a table as a grenade bounced toward them. Crimson kicked it mid-air, sending it flying back into its thrower’s horrified face.

The explosion sent wooden debris spiraling toward Navi. She didn't flinch. She moved within the chaos, each shard of wood and glass falling around her like snowflakes in a lethal blizzard—none daring to harm her. Instead, they sheltered her, guided her movements, letting her step between their deadly edges, unscathed.

Bullets crisscrossed the air, the two warriors weaving between them in a deadly tango. Enemies began firing wildly, panicked, unable to comprehend the impossibility of their failure. Navi caught a brief lull, ducking behind Crimson as he fired relentlessly, his body a wall, his heart a shield.

A soldier rushed forward with a blade, slicing at Crimson’s unguarded side. Navi’s knife met his throat first, a flash of silver lightning. Crimson didn't look back. He didn't need to.

The battlefield began to thin, the survivors hesitating, fear creeping into their hearts as they watched the dance unfold—beautiful, terrifying, impossible. They'd fought countless battles, seen death and destruction, but never had they faced opponents who moved as one, a single force of nature.

Navi and Crimson locked eyes once more, a brief, fierce moment that spoke louder than words ever could. They were no longer two separate beings. They were a single soul, a single purpose.

With a shared breath, they surged forward. The remaining enemies scattered, fleeing into the darkness or falling before them, their resistance broken. Silence descended, heavy and deafening.

They stood alone, weapons smoking, bodies unscathed. Around them lay devastation, a testament to a dance never rehearsed yet performed to perfection.

Crimson exhaled slowly, the rush of adrenaline giving way to awe. Navi stepped close, hand reaching out to touch his chest, feeling the rapid heartbeat beneath her palm. Their eyes met, understanding passing silently between them.

"We've never done that before," Navi whispered, breathless.

Crimson smiled faintly, gripping her hand tightly. "No, but maybe we were always meant to."

As the dust settled, they stood together, undefeated, invincible in that moment. Destiny had spoken, and its voice was clear.

They were unstoppable.

**Chapter 10: ECHOES IN THE DARK**

The cantina wreckage smoldered under the twin moons of Therion-5. The air, thick with the acrid scent of plasma burns and spilled synth-liquor, carried the last echoes of the gunfight. Somewhere in the rubble, a twisted neon sign flickered—a half-lit warning in some forgotten dialect, buzzing feebly against the silence.

Crimson exhaled, letting the adrenaline taper off as he pressed his back against the remains of the bar, his pulse still hammering in his ears. His left arm ached—a deep, bone-level throb from where a lucky shot had grazed him. He glanced sideways at Navi, who sat across from him, her frame bathed in the flickering orange glow of a broken lantern. The firelight danced in her eyes, reflecting something unreadable.

For a long moment, neither of them spoke.

Then, Navi broke the silence, her voice softer than he’d ever heard it. “You hesitated.”

Crimson scoffed. “I call it prioritizing.”

She tilted her head, strands of loose silver hair falling over her shoulder, catching the light like spun filament. “Prioritizing what?”

His jaw tightened. She knew exactly what. He had her in his crosshairs first. When the last of the mercs had drawn their final breath, she had still been standing, untouched, yet he had turned away first, checking the perimeter, making sure *she* wasn’t hurt.

She had noticed.

Navi’s fingers traced the edge of a cracked dataslate she had pulled from the leader’s remains. “This,” she murmured, turning the slate’s fractured screen toward him. “It’s encrypted, but one word keeps repeating. ‘ECHO.’”

Crimson frowned, shifting closer. Their shoulders brushed, the warmth of her presence stark against the cool night. He took the slate, scrolling through the distorted text, watching as the word glitched in and out of static-laden files.

“ECHO,” he echoed. “Could be a name. A place. A call sign.”

“It’s a lead.”

The words should have ignited his focus, should have lit the next fire in his gut. Instead, all he could see was the way her fingers trembled slightly, the subtle tightness in her posture—the way she was gripping the slate like a lifeline.

They had barely spoken about her grandfather. About what had happened on the station. About the bullet wounds in history that no one ever really talked about, but that bled into every step forward.

Crimson exhaled, setting the slate down, letting it rest between them like an unspoken truth. “DayTown.”

Navi blinked, her gaze snapping up to meet his.

He rolled his shoulders, wincing slightly. “If we’re gonna figure this out, we do it right. Back at the apartment. Gear up. Research. And…” His lips quirked slightly. “Maybe, for just a moment, we let ourselves breathe.”

She searched his face, scanning for hesitation, for cracks in the resolve. But what she found was something else. A promise. A pause between the storms.

For the first time since the battle, she smiled. Just slightly. Just enough.

—

**DayTown, 7000km away.**

The skyline shimmered against the midnight glow, the city’s neon veins pulsing in rhythmic bursts. High above the streets, on the thirty-second floor of a glass-and-steel monolith, their apartment hummed with quiet energy. Holographic interface screens hovered mid-air, scattered with data, reports, fragments of information about ECHO.

But neither of them were looking at the screens. Not yet.

Navi sat cross-legged on the floor, flipping through an old book—*an actual, physical book.* Crimson leaned against the desk, watching her, fascinated. The way she traced the pages with her fingers, as if deciphering something older than both of them.

“You keep books,” he mused. “Didn’t peg you for the analog type.”

She arched a brow. “Some things are worth preserving.”

His gaze softened. “Yeah.”

Silence stretched between them, but it wasn’t the same as before. This wasn’t battle-worn quiet. This was… something else. Something softer. Something that lived in the spaces between words.

He stepped forward, crouching down, resting his arms on his knees. “We’re chasing ghosts, Navi.”

She smirked. “You scared?”

“No.” He reached out, brushing a stray silver strand from her face, letting his fingers linger against her skin. “Not as long as I have you.”

Her breath caught, just for a second. And then, slowly, she leaned in.

For one night—just one night—there were no missions. No encrypted names. No ghosts.

Just them.
