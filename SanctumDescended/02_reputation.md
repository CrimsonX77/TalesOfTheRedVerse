# THE CATHEDRAL UNDERNEATH
### Chapter Two: What the Town Says When You're Not Listening

---

Word moved fast in Thresh, and it moved faster when it had Crimson Valentine's name attached to it.

By the time he'd stepped out for parts — coolant line, a replacement bearing, and the honest hope that Old Ferris's shop had both in stock instead of one and a lie about the other — the street already seemed to know something was stirring. Not the specifics. Thresh never got the specifics. Just the shape of it, the way a town like this always sensed a departure coming, the same way it sensed weather.

"Heading out again?" Ferris asked, not looking up from the parts drawer he was digging through, though his voice had that particular careful neutrality people used around Crimson — respectful, wary, the tone you'd use for a dog that had never bitten anyone you personally knew, but which you'd heard stories about.

"Maybe."

"Mm." Ferris found the bearing, set it on the counter, didn't reach for the coolant line yet. "You take Sable?"

"Wouldn't leave without her."

"Good." A pause. "You be careful out there. Wherever *there* is."

Outside, two women stood near the noodle stall, close enough that Crimson caught the tail end of their conversation without meaning to.

"—swear he's been in and out of that shop three times, packing crates. Something's got him moving."

"Good. Man like that needs somewhere to point himself, or he just sits there and *simmers*."

"You've met him?"

"Once. Fixed my brother's rig for free after the crash on the flats. Didn't say two words the whole time. Just fixed it and left." A beat. "My mother used to point him out when I was a girl. Told me if a man ever looked at me the way he looks at *engines*, I was to run the other direction and not stop."

The other woman laughed, low. "And did you?"

"Didn't have to. He's never looked at anybody that way that I've seen. Except maybe her." A nod, unmistakably, toward the direction of the workshop. Toward Sable.

Further down, outside the drinking hall, a pair of off-duty district guards were doing what off-duty guards always did, which was talk too loud about things they shouldn't. One of them, younger, newer to Thresh, was asking questions the older one clearly found tiresome to answer.

"—no, I'm serious, what's the deal with that guy? Everyone gets weird when his name comes up."

"You weren't here for the incident with the freight cartel two years back."

"What incident?"

"Exactly." The older guard didn't elaborate. Didn't need to. He just took a long drink and looked, briefly, in the direction Crimson had gone, the way you'd glance at a fault line you knew was there but had learned to build around anyway. "Man doesn't raise his voice. That's the part that should scare you. He doesn't need to. Whatever he needs said, he says once, real quiet, and then it's done being a problem."

"That's it? That's the whole story?"

"That's the whole story anybody's willing to tell you. Ask him yourself if you want the rest. I wouldn't."

Crimson heard all of it — every word, the way he heard most things in Thresh whether he meant to or not — and gave none of it any weight beyond a faint, private, entirely humorless smile as he carried his parts back toward the shop. Reputation was just weather too, out here. You didn't argue with it. You just let it happen around you and kept walking.

Sable was waiting on the step when he got back, arms crossed, watching him with the specific look of someone who'd clearly caught at least some of the same overheard commentary drifting through the street.

"Mothers warning their daughters about you again?"

"Every week, like clockwork."

"You love it."

"I tolerate it," he said, echoing himself from an hour before, and let her take half the crates out of his arms without either of them commenting on it. "There's a difference."

"Sure there is." She fell into step beside him toward the shop door. "For the record, for what it's worth — I've heard the stories. All of them. The ones people whisper and the ones they flat-out refuse to say out loud."

"And?"

"And I still let you carry the good coolant line," she said, "so draw your own conclusions."

---

Preparing to leave Thresh was, as always, ninety percent argument and ten percent actual packing.

"We are not," Crimson said, for what felt like the fourth time, "bringing the plasma cutter."

"It's *light*."

"It's not light, it's heavy, and you only ever want it along so you can cut something dramatically in half at some point, and I'm telling you now, whatever's out there, it is not going to need to be cut dramatically in half."

"You don't know that."

"I know that we are going to be thousands of kilometers from the nearest charge station, and the cutter draws power like it's got a grudge against the concept of conservation."

Sable set it down with the theatrical reluctance of someone conceding a point she still believed she was winning, and moved on to the next crate. Between them, the gear laid out across the workshop floor had the specific chaos of an expedition that hadn't fully decided what it was expecting to find: rations, rope, a portable scanner unit Crimson had rebuilt himself out of three broken ones, medkits, a tent rated for weather considerably worse than Thresh ever produced, spare parts for the transport, and — tucked in almost as an afterthought, wedged between a coil of cable and a box of flares — Ech0's favorite blanket, because nobody had needed to discuss whether she was coming.

She wasn't, technically, coming *along* so much as coming *regardless*, the same way weather comes regardless. She'd already claimed the transport's passenger seat an hour ago and had not moved since, watching the packing process with the mild, proprietary interest of a supervisor who fully intended to take credit for a job well done without contributing to it.

"You checked the coordinates again?" Sable asked, hauling the last crate toward the transport's cargo hold.

"Three times." Crimson pulled up the map on his wrist display — the same blank stretch of nothing Halvard had sent over, no road markers, no settlement pins, no survey data, a hole in the record where a thousand kilometers of country should have had *something*. "Whatever's out there, nobody's catalogued it. Not corp, not gov. Not even the old salvage networks have a whisper of it."

"That doesn't worry you?"

"Everything worries me a little. That's how you stay alive out here." He closed the display, looked over the transport — solid, patched in a dozen places, but his, every weld his own work — and felt the particular quiet settle over him that always came right before a departure. Not nerves exactly. More like the moment before diving into cold water, where your whole body knows what's coming and braces for it anyway. "But no. Not more than usual."

The workshop, behind them, sat in its familiar half-organized ruin — the two disassembled mechs, the drone still waiting on its overdue part, the towers of scrap leaning at their questionable angles. And at the back, still under its tarp, still and silent and gold beneath the dust, the old one.

Crimson didn't look at it as he did his final walkthrough. He'd gotten good at that.

But as he passed, for just a moment, he slowed his step — not quite stopping, not quite acknowledging — and said, low enough that Sable, already climbing into the transport, didn't hear it.

"Watch the place while I'm gone."

Nothing answered. Nothing ever did.

He kept walking.

---

The last thing Thresh gave them, as the transport pulled out past the district line and the low rooftops gave way to open scrubland, was Ferris standing in his doorway, arms crossed, watching them go with an expression that wasn't quite worry and wasn't quite anything else Crimson had a name for.

He didn't wave. Thresh wasn't a waving kind of town.

He just watched until the transport was small enough to lose in the dust, and then went back inside to a shop that would, for a while, be missing its strangest and most reliable customer.

Behind them, the rooftops shrank to a smudge on the horizon. Ahead, the road — what road there was — unspooled into scrubland, and then, somewhere beyond the edge of anyone's map, into a thousand kilometers of country nobody had bothered to write down.

Ech0 pressed her nose to the window glass and watched Thresh disappear.

She did not look away until it was completely gone.

---

*End of Chapter Two.*
