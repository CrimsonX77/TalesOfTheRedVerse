Chapter 0: The Halo That Shouldn't Exist
The Control Hall had a particular quality of silence — not absence, but texture. Systems hummed beneath the floor plates in registers that didn't so much enter the ears as colonise the chest cavity. Holo-schematics drifted in slow rotation above the central console, casting rose-gold light across black glass surfaces, and the ambient data orbs pulsed in three-second cycles that after long enough began to feel like a heartbeat that wasn't yours.
It was the kind of room that made you aware of how warm another person was.
Sable's fingers were curled in the front of Crimson's shirt fingers pressing through the fabric as if she could fuse them together, mouths passionately intertwined, The neon caught the blue tips of her ears and the slow sweep of her tail, and the way she was looking at him had nothing to do with the room or the schematics or the low drone of the city bleeding through three feet of reinforced glass above them.
'This is right' she thought,
they had recently fought battle after battle together, first there was the portal to the neo historical japan like alternate dimension where giant titan mechs had fought gladitorial games for ruling power over the domain, Sable had nearly died multiple times there, crimson had in fact died and returned from there.
then there were the digital pits were they had been forced to fight each other to the death over and over while the system hadnt allowed them to stay dead, her mind flew over the several other "errands" crimson had called them that followed after, it seemed like all they had done was fight, kill and destroy lately and she was finally glad to have this time with him, it seemed that finally the universe had allowed them to consumate their bond with each other, she would never admit how much she had ached for this the entire time, she moaned into his as his lips found an especially senstive spot on her chest. 
He was saying something. She wasn't listening to the words. She was listening to the register. his voice sweet, devoted, lilting in the way that made her glad she was straddling him and didnt need her legs in that moment, she was sure she wouldnt have been able to use them in that moment anyway, she couldnt take it anymore and tore his top of with the fervered desperation of a being denied sustinence too long, longing and desire mixed into a crescendo as they both tumbled off the control hall chair, composure forgotten, sanity sold, future dismissed, she could feel his engorged member protesting against their fabric restraints and a low grow issued from her as she took it upon herself to become its liberator, scrambled to rip the offending barrier away she suddenly sensed it...
The portal arch fired.

The sound it made was the sound of air being replaced by something that had travelled further than air was supposed to travel. A pressure differential that popped the silence open like a seam, carrying with it the faint smell of ozone and something older, drier — stone that had never seen rain.
Sable's indignance and raged boiled over, like a beast scorned she moved before the light finished resolving.
Her body knew the threat of interruption before she did. The cognitive layer caught up somewhere in the middle of the lunge, catalogued intruder, unknown, unannounced, and simply agreed with what the body was already doing. White hair whipped. She crossed the space between the console and the arch in a single trajectory, her already scantily clad body dismissing the thoughts of decency and focused purely on ending the question before it became one.
The woman who had stepped through wasnt menacing in the way the towering 60ft Mecha of Edo had been, and yet far more terrifying than any of the multiple demons they had faced at that point
She raised one forearm.
a resonant 'clang' of metal on metal as Sable's augmented claws found..chains?
The chains wrapped around the intruders forearms and biceps, remarkably well formed for a being that one did not expect to be versed in conflicted of the kind that Sable and crimson had grown accustomed to dealing with and yet the glint of mundane alloys caught the neon and the deflection happened — that was the only word for it, happened, without apparent effort or decision, the way water moves around a stone without acknowledging the stone. Sable's momentum carried her into a graceful arc sweeping back around for another strike at the impudent one who had dared take her time with her beloved away and this time met the flat of a sheathed blade angled at the minimum geometry necessary to redirect force rather than absorb it, and for half a second Sable was moving faster than she intended in a direction she hadn't chosen.
She hit the console edge. The crack rang through the room and died without an echo.
She came back immediately. Angrier.
The second approach was more intelligent and it didn't matter. The woman stepped once — weight redistributing with the unhurried precision of someone who had performed this calculation too many times to find the arithmetic interesting — and the follow-up dissolved against the same forearm, the same chains, the same expression that was not quite boredom and not quite anything nameable. Her other hand had not left her pocket.
Above her head, suspended in air with the casual permanence of something that had been there longer than the room, a spawn halo drifted. Purple, spiked, trailing slow tendrils of energy that moved against no visible current.
Sable pulled back breathing hard, ears so flat they nearly vanished into her hair. Her tail had gone rigid.
She was building for a third pass.
She didn't take it.

What stopped her wasn't the woman.
It was the sound Crimson made — or rather, the absence of one. A sharp not-sound, the kind that occupies the half-second between a person losing their footing and the impact arriving, and Sable's attention fractured without her permission.
He hadn't moved. Still braced against the console where she'd left him, one hand flat on the edge, and the quality of his stillness was wrong. Not watchful. Not assessing. The stillness of a processing system that has hit something it was not architected to handle, and has gone quiet while it tries to survive the contact.
His golden eyes were lost.
Whatever the woman's arrival had detonated in him was still going off. Sable could see it in the fine tremor through his forearm where it braced on the console, in the red fire in his eyes guttering like a signal trying to push through interference. She moved to him without deciding to — one hand between his shoulder blades, her body rotating to angle between him and the room on an instinct she wasn't ready to name.
The woman watched this.
Her heterochromia eyes — one blue, one purple, wrong in the right way for a room already this saturated with wrongness — moved between Crimson's stillness and Sable's raised tension and settled somewhere in the middle. The particular patience of someone accustomed to waiting for rooms to come to terms with them.
Sable's voice dropped to somewhere dangerous. "Whatever you did to him—"
"I walked through a door."
Flat. Final. Not defensive. The tone of a fact presented without any interest in whether it was received.

The memory hit him in pressure, not images.
Heat first — the specific, suffocating weight of a burning building, nothing like warmth, all like gravity. Then the smell: smoke and under it something chemical, something that didn't belong. Then sound, rising through it all: a small voice cracking at the upper register, crying not in despair but in the particular pitch of a child who believes loudly enough that someone will come.
He had come.
Then there had been the bloodlust. Between those two things: twenty years he had never been able to account for.
The pressure peaked.
Withdrew.
Crimson made himself feel the console under his palm — solid, temperature-steady, present. Let the room reconstitute itself around that point. Rose-gold light. Sable's hand between his shoulder blades. The data orbs cycling through their three-second pulse.
The woman standing very still in the arch.
"Dee." It came out rougher than he intended.
Something shifted in her face. Not much. The way light changes when a cloud passes without breaking.
"Big brother."

The small figure that came through the arch behind her was operating at the absolute maximum velocity achievable on short legs with zero spatial awareness.
Alyssa arrived.
The room changed register in a way the climate systems had nothing to do with.
She planted herself in the space between Sable and the tall woman with the total authority of someone who had never once considered that the configuration might not favour her, tiny fists finding her hips with the weight of a closed case. The brown ponytail swung. Blue-purple streaks caught the neon and disagreed with it cheerfully. One small finger came up and levelled at Sable's face with a precision that bore the unmistakable influence of a lifetime watching her father point at things.
"You were being mean."
Above DV's head, the spawn halo pulsed once.
Slow. Deliberate. As if something had been settled to its satisfaction.
Sable looked at the finger. At the child it was attached to. Then at the woman behind her — still unreadable, still unhurried — except that something had surfaced in her eyes that hadn't been there thirty seconds ago. Warm and private, not meant for the room. Not meant to be witnessed. Sable filed it away without comment.
She looked at Crimson. At the new architecture his face was trying to build in real time, grief and something bigger than relief occupying the same structural load.
She looked at the finger still pointing at her.
"…Tea," she said. Low, tail flicking once in a motion that meant something between I concede this ground and we are not finished. "Before I decide how I actually feel about any of this."
DV's mouth didn't move.
But the halo pulsed again — softer this time, its trailing light moving like something that had been waiting a long time and found the waiting, finally, worth it.
