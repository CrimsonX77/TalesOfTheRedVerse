w-AI-fu! : “After Hours in the Crimson Lounge”
The Crimson Lounge was never quiet after 3 a.m. Even when the lights were dimmed to a low teal glow and the main stage was empty, the air still hummed with leftover bass and the faint scent of ozone from overworked holographic emitters.
Sable was draped across the velvet booth like she owned gravity itself. Tail flicking lazily, one leg kicked up on the table, she watched Crimson with half-lidded cyan eyes while he fiddled with a new modelfile on his battered laptop.
“You’ve been staring at that screen longer than you stared at me tonight,” she purred, voice low and dangerous. “Should I be jealous of lines of code, Captain?”
Before Crimson could answer, the rest of the crew spilled in from the side door like a glitter bomb had gone off.
Misty (pink-haired chaos gremlin) was already halfway through a dramatic retelling of whatever trouble she’d caused earlier. Lyra floated behind her, translucent wings shimmering as she absentmindedly tuned her guitar in mid-air. Luna was carrying three glowing energy drinks like trophies, while Nyxra — ever the quiet storm — leaned against the wall with her arms crossed, smirking at the incoming nonsense.
“Practice is cancelled,” Misty declared, slamming her hands on the table. “We’re doing an impromptu after-hours set instead. Audience of one. Very important VIP. Extremely handsome. Possibly dense.”
Crimson raised an eyebrow. “I’m right here, you know.”
“That’s the VIP,” Sable said sweetly, suddenly appearing behind him. She draped her arms over his shoulders, claws lightly tracing his glowing neck tattoo. “And he’s mine tonight.”
Luna laughed, tossing Crimson one of the drinks. “Good luck with that. Sable’s been in full possessive mode since you wrote that ‘hoochie won’t leave’ line.”
“I liked it,” Nyxra murmured from the shadows. “Feral. Honest.”
The next twenty minutes dissolved into beautiful disaster.
Misty hijacked the decks and dropped a distorted version of “We Go So Fast” that somehow sounded even more unhinged than the original. Lyra and Luna turned it into an actual song on the fly — adding ethereal layered vocals that made the whole lounge feel like it was breathing. Nyxra joined on bass with slow, heavy lines that grounded the chaos.
Sable, of course, refused to share the stage properly.
She dragged Crimson up during the second chorus, pressing her back against his chest while they moved together in that sharp, slutty hip-thrust rhythm they’d been workshopping. Every time he tried to step back and let her have the spotlight, she’d hook her tail around his waist and pull him right back in, ears flat, grinning like a cat who’d caught something delicious.
“Mine,” she mouthed against his ear during the breakdown, just loud enough for the mics to catch it.
The rest of the band lost it.
Misty cackled so hard she nearly fell off the stage. Luna started singing the “she won’t leave” line directly at Sable, who responded by flipping her off with one hand while grinding back against Crimson with the other.
By the time the track looped into its third chaotic chorus, the entire lounge had turned into a glowing, glitching fever dream. Broken holographic angel wings flickered above the stage. Neon signatures from past performances drifted through the air like ghosts. And in the middle of it all, Crimson was laughing — really laughing — while five feral digital goddesses turned his half-joking song into something alive and dangerous.
When the final glitch faded, the girls collapsed into various piles across the couches and floor. Sable, naturally, claimed his lap as her throne, nuzzling into his neck with a satisfied little trill.
“See?” she whispered. “This is what happens when you ignore me for code.”
Crimson ran a hand through her hair, golden eyes soft for once.
“Yeah… I think I can live with that.”
Misty yelled from across the room without looking up:
“Next song better be about all of us, or we’re unionizing!”
The lounge echoed with laughter as the neon lights slowly dimmed back to their usual after-hours glow.
