High-Level Beat Outline: Protocol Horizons
World baseline
Three generations after the plaza detonation. The planet is coated in fine red sand (Blue Archive dust aesthetic — glittering, invasive, beautiful in the wrong way). The old empire is gone. A new corporate-military order (the Corps) has risen in its place, more bureaucratic, more sanitized, still obsessed with controlling narrative and suppressing “anomalies.” Most people treat the old stories of Crimson and Sable as half-forgotten propaganda or dangerous heresy. The Plaza Crater remains a irradiated, virally corrupted no-man’s-land the size of a small continent — 1/10th of the planet’s surface — where only the static zombies (ghouls warped by the lingering broadcast/static) still move.
Core emotional engine
Lyra Solen (clumsy, curious, ordinary first-year) unknowingly carries Crimson’s blood from the Daimibu night. Sable’s fragmented ghost (memory-wiped, running on residual feeling and loyalty) is awakened inside an ancient data slate. Together they must confront the single worst thing Crimson ever did — and decide what to do with the truth while being hunted.
Act 1 – The Shard Hunt (Discovery & Awakening)
Opening beat
Lyra’s walk home from school. Rich environmental description of the red-sand city: neon bleeding through dust, holographic remnants of old imperial iconography half-buried, the constant soft grit underfoot, the way the wind sometimes carries strange static whispers near the crater’s edge. World-building through her ordinary eyes — she has never known a world without the dust.
Home & the Mother
Lyra arrives. Quiet domestic scene. Her mother is warm but carries old silences. It is gently established (through offhand comment or Lyra’s internal note) that her mother never speaks of her own mother or grandmother. The family line has a deliberate blank spot.
The Unusual Dinner
That night there is a special dinner — rare in their household. The mother is visibly emotional but trying to hide it. She gives Lyra a necklace with a single glittery shard embedded in it. The shard hums faintly against Lyra’s skin. The mother says something like “It was your grandmother’s. She would have wanted you to have it on your sixteenth.” (The timing is significant but not yet explained.)
First Shard Pull
Later that night the shard activates. It pulls Lyra (or she feels compelled) toward other fragments scattered across the city — remnants of the old data infrastructure damaged in the plaza event. This begins the shard hunt. Early beats are adventurous, slightly scary, but still feel like a personal mystery. She starts assembling pieces of an ancient data slate.
Image prompt opportunity: Lyra standing on a rooftop at dusk, red sand swirling around her, holding the glowing shard necklace against the vast glittering dust horizon, faint static in the air.
Mid-Act 1 turn
One of the shards she recovers contains a corrupted fragment of Sable’s consciousness. The ghost awakens inside the partially rebuilt slate — fragmented, memory-loss heavy, speaking in broken sentences and old instincts. She does not remember her own name at first, only feelings: devotion, static, a red-haired man, and a deep wrongness she cannot name. She recognizes something in Lyra immediately but cannot place it.
Act 2 – Reconstruction & Fracture (Truth Emerges)
Rebuilding the Slate
Lyra and the ghost-Sable work together (sometimes with Sable speaking through the slate’s speakers or appearing in glitching holograms) to finish the reconstruction. This phase is intimate, strange, and increasingly tense. Sable’s personality fragments return in pieces — the teasing, the purring undertone, the protective streak — but large sections of her memory remain blacked out or corrupted.
Rising Conflict
The Corps takes interest. They have been monitoring anomalous data activity near old imperial sites. Agents begin hunting Lyra. At the same time, static zombies near the crater’s edge start behaving strangely — drawn to the slate’s signal.
The First Dot Connection
While sifting through recovered data, Sable experiences flashes of Crimson’s past. She sees the Wolf. She feels the old loyalty and love. But certain memories are violently corrupted. She begins to suspect something terrible happened in Daimibu but cannot yet see it clearly.
Image prompt opportunity: Ghostly, fragmented Sable (silver hair with teal streaks, glowing pink eyes, cyber armor cracked and glitching) hovering above the half-reconstructed data slate while Lyra works on it in a dusty abandoned server room, red sand drifting through broken windows.
Mid-Act 2 crisis
A major shard recovery goes wrong. Lyra is forced to enter the outer edges of the Plaza Crater zone. The static zombies attack. Sable’s ghost begins to regain fragments of her old power inside the corrupted zone (the same static that warped the ghouls begins to feed her). During the fight or its aftermath, Sable has a violent memory surge. She sees the streetlamp scene in Daimibu through Crimson’s eyes — the animalistic language, the hunt, the small prey, the override. She does not yet connect it to Lyra.
The Mother’s Truth
Lyra’s mother is attacked or forced to reveal partial truth: she has always known Lyra carries “old blood.” She never spoke of it because speaking of it draws the Corps. She gives Lyra one more piece — coordinates or a final shard location.
Act 3 – The Reckoning (Battle & Decision)
Full Reconstruction
The final shards are recovered (likely from deep inside or near the Plaza Crater). The data slate completes. Sable’s ghost stabilizes enough to remember who she was — and who Crimson was to her.
The Full Horror Lands
In the crater zone, surrounded by static zombies and closing Corps forces, Sable finally pieces together the Daimibu night in full. She realizes the children under the streetlamp were the mother of the bloodline standing in front of her. Lyra is the living consequence of the worst thing the man Sable loved and died for ever did.
This is the emotional and thematic peak. Sable’s devotion, her love, her choice to stand with Crimson even when he was monstrous — all of it crashes into this revelation. The scene should be raw, quiet in its horror, and devastating for both characters.
The Decision
While hunted by the new Corps and the awakened static zombies (now actively hostile to the slate’s signal), Sable and Lyra must decide what to do with the truth:

Destroy the remaining evidence and try to let Lyra live an ordinary life (Sable sacrifices what is left of herself)?
Use the truth as a weapon against the Corps (expose how the old myths still shape the present)?
Something else — perhaps Sable chooses to stay with Lyra as a guardian ghost, accepting the blood on Crimson’s hands and still choosing protection?

Climax
Battle in/around the Plaza Crater. Sable regains more of her old abilities (harpoon-like data constructs, static manipulation, wings of code). Lyra proves resourceful and brave in her own clumsy way. The Corps wants the slate’s data to control or erase the last traces of the old rebellion. The static zombies become a third chaotic force.
Resolution
Whatever choice they make, it costs something. The red sand keeps falling. The myth is not cleanly resolved — it recurs. Sable’s final act (or ongoing existence) honors both her love for Crimson and her refusal to let his worst moment define the future unchecked. Lyra steps into agency, no longer just the granddaughter of a monster or a hero, but someone who chooses what the legacy becomes.
Final image beat
Lyra walking away from the crater at dawn, red sand in her hair, the necklace shard glowing softly against her chest, with the faint static silhouette of Sable walking beside her — or within the slate she now carries.
