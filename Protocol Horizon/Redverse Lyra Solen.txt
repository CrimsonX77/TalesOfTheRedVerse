[Protocol Horizon]

Redverse: Lyra Solen

Three generations after the world was reshaped by a mysterious cataclysm known as the Red Eclipse(The events of Mythopunk), humanity survives in a landscape coated with endless red sand—dusted over skyscrapers, streets, and neon-lit megacities alike. The event left echoes of a rebel whose name is almost forgotten, but whose actions still scar every corner of civilization.

Lyra Solen is an ordinary first-year high schooler, clumsy and curious, who has never known the world before the dust fell. But tonight, something feels different. As she navigates the glittering chaos of her city—the neon-lit halls, the holographic student art, the winds carrying fine, ruby-colored sand—she begins to sense that the past is more alive than anyone admits.

When fragments of forgotten history and subtle signs of an old rebellion begin to surface, Lyra must decide what her place in this scarred world will be. Will she merely follow the paths set by those who came before, or will she carve her own story through the dust?

A tale of mystery, legacy, and resilience, Redverse: Lyra Solen explores a world where even ordinary life is colored by the echoes of legend—and where one young girl may discover how far the past still reaches.


[Chapter 1]
The locker door rattled like it always did in empty rooms. Except this time, the room wasn’t empty.

Lyra Solen, first-year high schooler and clumsy for her age, was gathering her things after swimming practice. Her mother had said tonight was supposed to be special. Lyra had no idea why.

The school halls glowed with magenta neon strips, marking paths to different sections. Holographic art pieces blinked and shimmered, each telling the stories of their creators as Lyra trod along the gangway of circuits and concrete.

Outside, the wind had picked up, whipping dust into miniature tornadoes along the streets. Lyra squinted through the haze. The red sand glittered like crushed rubies, coating the buildings, the streets, even the neon signage. Everywhere she looked, the world was the same: red sand stretching as far as the eye could see.

They say it wasn’t always like this, she mused, brushing a handful of coarse grains off her sleeve. That “incident” changed everything… What would it have been like before?

She laughed softly, the sound lost in the whispering wind. Somehow, she suspected the answer would be… boring.
