# THE WORLD DOMINATION AGENCY LTD™
## Episode Two: "The Auditor"

---

### COLD OPEN

The filing cabinet was older than the building.

Lyra noticed it on her third day — really noticed it, beyond the initial impression of "duct tape and optimism." The metal was different. Heavier. Military-grade, maybe, though she didn't know how she knew that. And the lock wasn't the cheap hardware-store variety you'd expect in a back-alley office.

It was biometric.

Hidden under a fake keyhole, but definitely biometric. She'd seen Max press his thumb to it when he thought she wasn't looking.

"Curiosity."

She jumped. Max was standing in the bathroom-kitchenette doorway, two mugs of questionable coffee in hand, watching her with an expression she couldn't quite read.

"What?"

"That's what kills cats." He handed her a mug. "Also Mr. Polaski, if Mrs. Henderson's threats are to be believed. She's quite protective of Mr. Whiskers now. I think she's enrolled him in self-defense classes."

"There are self-defense classes for cats?"

"There are self-defense classes for everything. This is New York." He settled into his chair, not looking at the filing cabinet. Very specifically not looking at it. "Did you know there's a dojo in Queens that teaches combat techniques to emotional support peacocks? I considered enrolling once, but they said my energy was 'too chaotic for Gerald.' Gerald is the peacock."

"Max."

"Yes?"

"What's in the filing cabinet?"

He took a long sip of coffee. The mug said **WORLD'S OKAYEST SUPERVILLAIN** in cheerful Comic Sans.

"Tax documents," he said.

"Bullshit."

"Language, Ms. Chen. We're a professional organization." But he was smiling — that real smile, the one that crinkled the corners of his eyes. "Ask me again in a month."

"Why a month?"

"Because by then, you'll either have decided I'm completely insane and fled to somewhere sensible, or you'll have decided I'm only *mostly* insane and committed to the bit." He raised his mug in a toast. "I'm hoping for the latter. You make excellent coffee."

"*You* make the coffee. I just drink it."

"Exactly. Your consumption technique is flawless. Very professional. I'm putting it in your performance review."

Lyra wanted to push. Every instinct she had screamed *push, demand, don't let him deflect with charm and chaos.*

But something stopped her.

Maybe it was the way he held himself when he thought she wasn't watching — the stillness that crept in, the thousand-yard stare that suggested he was seeing something very far away. Maybe it was the rooftop, the way he'd shifted from absurd to terrifying in the space of a breath.

Maybe it was the simple fact that he'd given her a job, a purpose, and exactly zero reasons to trust him except for the inexplicable certainty that she did.

"One month," she said.

"One month," he agreed.

The filing cabinet sat in the corner, patient and silent, keeping its secrets.

---

### ACT ONE: THE VISITOR

The family friend arrived on a Tuesday.

No warning. No appointment. Just the sound of the alley door slamming open hard enough to rattle the motivational poster, and then a voice like gravel wrapped in disappointment:

"Maximilian. What the *hell* did you do?"

Lyra looked up from the client intake forms she'd been organizing (they had clients now, actual clients, word apparently traveled fast in the "problems too weird for normal solutions" community) to see a woman in her late fifties stalking into the office.

She was terrifying.

Not in the way Max was terrifying — that cold, precise danger that lived underneath the chaos. This was different. This was the terror of someone who had seen every possible version of your bullshit and was no longer impressed by any of it.

Steel-gray hair, cropped short. A face that had probably been beautiful once and was now something better: *formidable*. She wore a coat that cost more than Lyra's last three apartments combined, and she moved like someone who had never once in her life been kept waiting.

"Aunt V!" Max's voice went up an octave. Actually up an octave. Lyra had never heard him sound like that. "What a delightful surpri—"

"Constantine called me."

The temperature in the room dropped.

"Ah," Max said.

"'Ah.' Yes. 'Ah.'" The woman — Aunt V — turned to look at Lyra for the first time. Her eyes were the color of a winter ocean. "And who is this?"

"Lyra Chen." Max's voice had steadied, but there was something careful in it now. Protective. "My Chief Administrative Officer of Global Operations."

"Your *receptionist.*"

"Aspirational titling. I explained this—"

"You explained nothing. You *vanished*, Maximilian. Two years. Not a word. And now I find out you're running some kind of—" She gestured at the office, the water stain, the filing cabinet, the poster. Her lip curled. "—*operation* in my building, antagonizing people who could have you killed with a phone call."

"Your building?" Lyra said, before she could stop herself.

Aunt V's gaze swung back to her. Heavy. Assessing.

"Victoria St. Claire-Pemberton," she said, not offering a hand. "Max's aunt. Owner of this building and approximately forty percent of the commercial real estate in a six-block radius. And you, Ms. Chen, are either very brave or very stupid to have attached yourself to my nephew's latest disaster."

"She's neither," Max said quietly. "She's *good*. Leave her out of this."

Something flickered across Victoria's face. Too fast to read.

"Out," she said.

"I'm sorry?"

"You. Ms. Chen. Out. I need to speak with my nephew privately."

Lyra looked at Max. He gave her the smallest nod.

"There's a café two blocks north," he said. "The one with the peacock mural. Tell them you're with the Agency and they'll give you the good croissants."

"The good—"

"Trust me."

She didn't want to leave. Every instinct screamed to stay, to hear what was about to be said, to understand.

But Max had asked her to go.

And for reasons she couldn't quite name, that was enough.

---

### INTERLUDE: CROISSANTS AND QUESTIONS

The café did have a peacock mural. Also a parrot named Leonard who sat on a perch by the register and judged everyone's orders.

"Agency," Lyra said to the barista, feeling absurd.

The barista — a young man with pink hair and a name tag that read DMITRI (HE/THEY) — lit up.

"Oh! You're with Max? He's the *best*. Fixed our rat problem last month. Well, I say 'fixed' — he actually just negotiated a territory agreement with them? They get the basement, we get the main floor, and nobody tells the health inspector. Very diplomatic." Dmitri slid a croissant across the counter. "On the house. Any friend of the Agency."

Lyra took the croissant. It was, objectively, the best croissant she had ever encountered.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Shoot."

"How long has Max been doing... this? The Agency thing?"

Dmitri's face did something complicated. "Maybe a year? Year and a half? He showed up one day, helped Mrs. Okafor with her landlord situation — don't ask, there were *curses* involved, like actual ones, we live above a very dramatic neighborhood — and word just kind of spread." They paused. "He's different from when he started, though."

"Different how?"

"Sadder?" Dmitri shook their head. "No, that's not right. *Deeper*. Like he's seen something he can't unsee, you know? Sometimes he comes in here and just... stares out the window. For hours. I don't charge him for the coffee anymore. Feels wrong somehow."

Leonard the parrot squawked: "MAXIMUM EFFORT."

"He taught Leonard that," Dmitri said. "Took him two months. I have no idea why."

Lyra ate her croissant and thought about a man who stared out windows and taught parrots movie quotes and negotiated treaties with rats.

A man whose aunt owned half the neighborhood and looked at him like he was both precious and broken.

A man who kept a military-grade filing cabinet locked with biometrics in a room that smelled like ambition that had given up.

*What happened to you?* she thought. *What are you trying to fix?*

She didn't have answers.

But she was starting to understand the shape of the questions.

---

### ACT TWO: FAMILY BUSINESS

When Lyra returned, the office was quiet.

Too quiet. The kind of quiet that followed storms.

Max was sitting at his desk, staring at nothing. The manic energy was gone. In its place: something heavy. Old. The look of a man who had just been reminded of things he'd rather forget.

Victoria was nowhere to be seen.

"She left," Max said, without being asked. "Told me I had two weeks to 'resolve the Constantine situation' or she'd stop protecting me."

"Protecting you from what?"

He laughed — a hollow sound. "From them, I suppose. From what I used to be. From the consequences of asking questions I shouldn't have asked."

Lyra sat down across from him. Waited.

The silence stretched.

"I wasn't always this," Max said finally, gesturing at the room, at himself. "The suits that don't fit. The chaos. The *jokes*." He looked at her, and his eyes were older than his face. "I used to be someone important, Lyra. Someone with resources. Connections. A family name that opened doors instead of closing them."

"What happened?"

"I invented something." His gaze drifted to the filing cabinet. Just for a second. "Something that scared the wrong people. And instead of understanding, instead of *listening*, they tried to make me disappear."

"Your family?"

"Some of them." His voice was flat. Careful. "Not Aunt V. She's the one who helped me get away. Gave me this building, this room, this chance to start over. She's the reason I'm still breathing."

"And Constantine?"

"A complication." Max stood abruptly, moving to the window. The light caught his profile — the angles of his face, the weight in his shoulders. "He works for people who work for people who would very much like to find me. He didn't know I was here until the rooftop. Now he does."

"So what do we do?"

"*We*?" He turned, something flickering in his expression. Surprise. Hope. "You don't have to be part of this. What I said before — the severance offer. It still stands. You could walk away. Should, probably."

Lyra thought about the croissant. About Dmitri and the rats and Leonard the parrot and a neighborhood that had adopted this strange, broken man as their own personal problem-solver.

She thought about the way he'd said *she's good, leave her out of this.*

"I didn't take this job to walk away at the first sign of complications," she said. "You promised to explain. You said one month. I'm holding you to that."

"And if the explanation is worse than you imagine?"

"Then I'll deal with it."

He studied her for a long moment. Something shifted in his face — the walls coming down, just a crack.

"There's someone coming," he said quietly. "Someone Constantine is sending. Not thugs this time. Someone who knows me. Knew me. From before."

"An enemy?"

Max smiled, and it was the saddest thing she'd ever seen.

"Worse," he said. "A friend."

---

### ACT THREE: THE AUDITOR

His name was Ellis Park.

He arrived on Thursday, two days after Victoria's visit, walking into the office like he belonged there. Like he'd always belonged there. Like the crooked sign and the water stain and the squeaky chair were all exactly what he'd expected.

"Max." His voice was warm. Gentle. The voice of someone who had practiced being non-threatening until it became a weapon. "You look terrible."

"Ellis." Max didn't stand. Didn't smile. "You look exactly the same."

"Benefits of clean living." Ellis settled into the chair across from Max's desk — the client chair, Lyra realized, which meant he was positioning himself as supplicant even though everything about his body language screamed *control*. "Is this her? The new one?"

"Lyra Chen," Max said flatly. "My colleague."

"Colleague. Right." Ellis turned to look at her, and Lyra felt the full weight of his attention. He was handsome in a forgettable way — medium height, medium build, brown hair, brown eyes. The kind of face you'd forget immediately after meeting. 

Which, she realized with a chill, was probably intentional.

"Ms. Chen." He smiled. "I'm Ellis Park. I used to work with Max, back when he was someone else. I'm what you might call an... auditor."

"He's a fixer," Max said. "When powerful people have problems they can't solve through legal channels, they call Ellis. He makes things go away. *People* go away."

"That's rather reductive."

"It's accurate."

The two men stared at each other. Something crackled between them — not quite hostility, but close. The energy of two people who knew each other's worst secrets and had decided not to use them. Yet.

"Constantine sends his regards," Ellis said finally. "He was quite shaken by your little display on the rooftop. The man you threatened has been having nightmares. Apparently, you said something about 'assembly.'"

"I was being poetic."

"You were being *you*." Ellis leaned forward. "Which is the problem, Max. You were supposed to disappear. Quietly. Forever. That was the deal. Victoria keeps you hidden, the family stops looking, and everyone pretends the prodigal son simply... wandered off."

"I was never good at doing what I was supposed to."

"No." Ellis sighed. "You weren't."

"So what now? You're here to bring me in? Make me 'go away' like your other problems?"

Ellis was quiet for a long moment.

"I'm here to warn you," he said finally. "Constantine isn't the threat. He's just the dog that caught a scent. The *family*, Max — your uncle, your father's old partners — they're paying attention now. And they don't just want you to disappear anymore."

"What do they want?"

Ellis glanced at the filing cabinet. The movement was subtle. Almost invisible.

But Lyra caught it.

And from the way Max's expression flickered, so did he.

"They want what you took," Ellis said. "What you *invented*. They've tried to recreate it, you know. For two years. The best minds money can buy, working day and night, and they can't even begin to understand how you did it." His voice dropped. "You were always the brilliant one, Max. The one who saw things the rest of us couldn't. They thought they could steal your work and throw you away. They were wrong."

"Good."

"It's not *good*." For the first time, Ellis's composure cracked. Something real underneath — something that looked almost like fear. "What you built — what you *did* — it changed things. People who encountered your work, they're not the same. Some of them haven't slept in months. Some of them can't stop *crying*. And the ones who went looking for answers, who tried to understand the mathematics behind it..." He swallowed. "They're not people anymore, Max. Not really."

Lyra felt ice creep down her spine. She thought about the biometric lock. The military-grade metal. The way Max sometimes stared at nothing, like he was seeing six things she couldn't.

*What did you invent?*

"Why are you really here, Ellis?" Max's voice was quiet. Dangerous.

"Because I owe you." Ellis stood. "Because when I was nothing but a scared kid with too many debts and nowhere to go, you saw something in me. Gave me a chance. And when everything fell apart, when they asked me to be the one who found you, I said yes because I knew that if I didn't, they'd send someone who wouldn't bother with warnings."

He moved toward the door, then paused.

"You have two weeks," he said. "Maybe less. They're coming, Max. And they won't stop until they have what you stole."

"I didn't steal anything."

"No." Ellis smiled sadly. "You did something worse. You created something they can't control. And powerful people *hate* what they can't control."

He left.

The office was silent.

Lyra stared at Max. Max stared at the filing cabinet.

"I think," she said slowly, "that one month might have been optimistic."

Max laughed — real this time, surprised out of him.

"Yeah," he said. "Yeah, maybe."

He crossed to the filing cabinet. Pressed his thumb to the hidden sensor. The lock clicked open.

"I was going to wait," he said, pulling open the top drawer. "Ease you into it. The filing system is... extensive."

Lyra stood. Moved closer.

The drawer was full of files. Hundreds of them. Names, dates, photographs, documents. A spider's web of information that looked like it spanned decades.

"This is how we fight," Max said. "Not with guns or threats or violence. With *knowledge*. Every favor owed. Every secret kept. Every piece of leverage that the powerful think is buried forever."

"You've been collecting this for years."

"For lifetimes." His voice was strange. Distant. "Longer than you'd believe."

She reached for a file at random. He caught her wrist.

"Not that one," he said softly. "Not yet. That one's... different."

She looked at the tab. Handwritten. A single word:

**PROMETHEUS**

"What is it?"

Max closed the drawer. Locked it.

"Ask me again in a month," he said. "If we live that long."

---

### TAG

That night, after Max had gone home (wherever home was; Lyra still didn't know), she sat alone in the office.

The filing cabinet hummed.

Not literally. There was no sound. But she could feel it — a presence, a weight, a gravitational pull toward secrets she wasn't supposed to know.

*What did you build, Max?*

*What did they do to you?*

*What did you do to yourself?*

She thought about Ellis's words. People who weren't people anymore. Mathematics that drove men mad. Something so powerful that an entire family had torn itself apart trying to possess it.

She thought about Max's eyes, old beyond his years, seeing things she couldn't.

*Lifetimes*, he'd said. *Longer than you'd believe.*

Lyra Chen had taken this job because nothing else made sense.

She was beginning to realize that maybe the job didn't make sense either.

And maybe — just maybe — that was exactly why she had to stay.
