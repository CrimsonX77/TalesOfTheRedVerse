# THE WORLD DOMINATION AGENCY LTD™
## Episode Five: "The Sister"

---

### COLD OPEN

The filing cabinet was humming louder than usual.

Lyra noticed it the moment she walked in — a low, persistent vibration that she felt more than heard, like standing too close to a generator. She'd learned not to ask about the cabinet. She'd learned not to look at it too long. But today, the sound had teeth.

Max was at his desk, staring at a letter. Not reading it. Staring at it. Like it might bite.

"Morning," she said carefully.

No response.

She set down her bag. Crossed to the coffee maker. Started the ritual — grounds, water, the precise measurements she'd learned made the difference between "coffee" and "whatever Max had been drinking before she arrived."

"Max?"

He looked up. His eyes were wrong. Not distant — *locked*. Like something behind them had seized up.

"I need to visit my sister," he said.

Lyra blinked. In four months of working for him, she'd never heard him mention family. She'd assumed — well, she wasn't sure what she'd assumed. That he'd sprung fully formed from the earth, maybe. That the network was his only kin.

"You have a sister?"

"I have a Constantine." He said the name like it cost him something. "She runs the family business. We don't... talk. Often."

"But you need to talk now?"

He held up the letter. She couldn't read it from across the room, but she could see the handwriting — elegant, precise, somehow aggressive.

"She has something I need. Information about a situation I can't resolve through normal channels." He set the letter down, smoothing it flat with fingers that weren't quite steady. "She's agreed to see me. Which means she wants something in return."

"That's how family works?"

His laugh was hollow. "That's how *our* family works."

He stood, grabbing his coat with more force than necessary.

"You're coming with me."

It wasn't a question.

---

### ACT ONE: THE DRIVE

They took a car. An actual car — not the subway, not walking, not any of the methods Lyra had come to associate with Max's movement through the city. A sleek black vehicle that had been waiting at the curb like it knew they were coming.

"Whose car is this?" she asked as they pulled away from the office.

"Mine."

"You have a car?"

"I have several cars." He was looking out the window, watching the city slide past. "I don't use them. Cars are cages. They separate you from the texture of things."

"Then why—"

"Because we're going somewhere that requires a certain... presentation." His jaw tightened. "Constantine respects power. Not kindness. Not connection. *Power*. If I show up on foot, she'll read it as weakness."

Lyra absorbed this.

"What does she do? The family business?"

"Officially? Import-export. Art acquisition. High-end security consulting." He turned to look at her, something flickering in his eyes. "Unofficially? She solves problems that can't be solved legally. For people who can afford to pay for discretion."

"Like you."

"No." The word was sharp. "Not like me. I help people who need help. Constantine helps people who have *leverage*. There's a difference."

"You're both problem-solvers."

"A surgeon and a butcher both use knives." He looked away again. "We grew up in the same house. We learned from the same teachers. But somewhere along the way, we chose different definitions of the word 'help.'"

The city was changing outside the windows. Taller buildings. Cleaner streets. The kind of neighborhood where money had weight and everyone could feel it.

"Max." Lyra's voice was careful. "Why are you bringing me?"

He was quiet for a long moment.

"Because Constantine will try to get in my head. She's very good at it. She knows which buttons to push, which wounds to reopen." He exhaled slowly. "And because you ground me. You remind me of who I chose to be, instead of who I was raised to become."

Lyra felt something twist in her chest.

"That's a lot of pressure."

"I know." He finally looked at her — really looked. "I'm sorry. You can wait in the car if you prefer. I shouldn't have assumed—"

"I'm coming."

The words came out before she could second-guess them.

Max studied her face. Whatever he saw there made something in his expression soften.

"Thank you."

---

### ACT TWO: THE ESTATE

The Constantine estate was obscene.

There was no other word for it. A sprawling mansion set back from a private drive, all stone and glass and architecture that screamed *we have more money than you will ever see*. The grounds were manicured with military precision. Statues dotted the landscape — classical figures, gods and monsters, all of them positioned to be seen from the approaching road.

"You grew up here?" Lyra asked, voice faint.

"Until I was seventeen." Max's face was stone. "Then I left. I don't come back unless I have to."

The car stopped at the front entrance. A man in a dark suit opened the door — security, Lyra realized, not a butler. The way he moved, the way his eyes tracked them, the subtle bulge under his jacket. Security.

They were escorted inside.

The interior was worse than the exterior. Cold marble, priceless art, the kind of aggressive minimalism that said *we could fill this space with anything, but we choose emptiness because we can*. Their footsteps echoed like accusations.

And then they entered the study, and Lyra understood everything.

Constantine was beautiful.

The same bone structure as Max. The same intensity in the eyes. But where Max was kinetic energy barely contained, Constantine was stillness. Control. She sat behind a desk that probably cost more than Lyra's apartment building, dressed in something black and sharp and expensive, and she looked at them like they were interesting specimens she hadn't quite classified yet.

"Baby brother." Her voice was honey over razor wire. "It's been too long."

"Constantine." Max's voice was flat. Controlled. Nothing like the warmth Lyra was used to hearing.

Constantine's eyes slid to Lyra. Assessed. Dismissed. Returned to Max.

"You brought a guest. How unlike you."

"She's my assistant."

"Is that what we're calling it?" Constantine smiled — a perfect, terrible smile. "Please. Sit. Let's discuss why you've finally deigned to visit."

---

### THE NEGOTIATION

Lyra sat slightly behind Max, trying to make herself small and observant. She didn't understand half of what they were discussing — references to people and events and debts she had no context for — but she understood the dynamic.

This was a war. A cold, quiet war fought with words instead of weapons. Every sentence was a move. Every pause was calculation.

Constantine had information Max needed — something about Meridian Holdings, about whoever was really behind the property acquisitions in his neighborhood. Max had something she wanted in return.

"The Yamazaki file," Constantine said, leaning back in her chair. "Full access. No redactions."

"No."

"Then we have nothing to discuss."

"The Yamazaki file is off-limits. It involves people who trusted me. I won't burn that trust for information I can probably acquire through other channels."

Constantine's smile sharpened. "Then why are you here?"

"Because acquiring it through other channels would take time I don't have." Max's voice was steady, but Lyra could see the tension in his shoulders. "I'm offering you the Rotterdam contacts instead. Full dossier. Verified intelligence."

"Rotterdam is useful but not essential." Constantine examined her nails, bored. "What else?"

"What do you actually want, Constantine?"

The question hung in the air. Something shifted in Constantine's expression — a flicker of something that might have been genuine, before the mask slid back into place.

"I want to know what happened to you," she said quietly. "After the operation. After the weapon. You disappeared for three years. When you came back, you were... different."

Lyra felt Max go rigid beside her.

"That's not on the table."

"Everything's on the table, baby brother. That's how negotiation works." Constantine leaned forward. "You're not the same person who left. I want to know why. I want to know what that thing did to you."

"Constantine—"

"Did it hurt? When they activated it? I've heard rumors. Some of the subjects screamed for days. Some of them never stopped screaming — they just got quieter about it." Her voice was soft, almost gentle. "Did you scream, Max? Did you see something that broke you? Is that why you came back and started *helping people* like some sort of—"

"Enough."

The word was ice. Max stood, and Lyra saw something she'd never seen before — rage, controlled so tightly it was almost invisible, but burning underneath like magma under stone.

"You'll get the Rotterdam contacts and the Dubrovnik shipping routes. In exchange, you'll tell me everything you know about Meridian Holdings and whoever's actually signing the checks. That's the deal. Take it or don't."

Constantine studied him for a long moment.

"You've changed," she said finally. "You used to be better at hiding when I hit a nerve."

"I used to care about hiding it."

Something flickered in Constantine's eyes. Surprise? Respect? Lyra couldn't tell.

"Deal," Constantine said. "But you should know — whoever's behind Meridian, they're not local. The money trails go offshore and then they just... stop. Like someone built a wall specifically to keep people like me from seeing what's on the other side."

"What do you know about their interest in the 47th and Vine intersection?"

"Nothing concrete. But I've heard whispers." Constantine stood, moving to a window. "Someone believes that location is important. Not for real estate. For something else. Something about *positioning*."

"Positioning for what?"

Constantine turned, and for the first time, Lyra saw something like uncertainty in her expression.

"That's the question, isn't it?" She smiled — a thinner smile this time, with less teeth. "Find out, and let me know. I have a feeling this is going to be interesting."

---

### THE INTERRUPTION

They were walking back to the car when it happened.

Lyra was half a step behind Max, still processing the conversation, her mind full of fragments she didn't know how to assemble — *the operation, the weapon, did you scream* — when she felt something close around her wrist.

Not something. Someone.

A hand. Large. Rough. Pulling.

"Hey there, pretty—"

She didn't see Max move.

One moment he was beside her. The next moment there was a sound — wet, precise, like a butcher's stroke — and the hand on her wrist went slack.

Lyra stumbled back. Looked down.

Three fingers lay on the cobblestones, still twitching.

The man attached to them — big, scarred, wearing the kind of cheap suit that screamed *hired muscle* — was staring at his hand with an expression of profound confusion. Like he was trying to solve a math problem that didn't make sense.

Then the pain hit.

He screamed.

Max stood between them, a blade in his hand that Lyra hadn't seen him draw, that she couldn't even identify the origin of. His face was utterly calm. Empty. Like a glacier considering whether to move.

"She's mine."

The words were cold. Absolute. Not a threat — a statement of fact. A law of physics being articulated for the first time.

"Don't you *dare* ever reach for her."

The man was on his knees now, clutching his ruined hand, blood pooling on the stones. His eyes were wild, uncomprehending.

"I was just— I didn't— Constantine said I could—"

"Constantine said you could collect. Off the books. Earn some extra." Max's voice was still ice. Still empty. "Constantine didn't tell you about me. That was her mistake. And yours."

The blade disappeared somewhere. Lyra didn't see where. Max turned to her, and for one terrible moment his eyes were still cold, still that glacier blue—

Then they weren't.

Then they were just Max again, horrified and gentle and reaching for her without touching, hovering at the edges of her space.

"Are you okay? Did he hurt you? I'm sorry, I should have— I didn't see him until he was already—"

"I'm fine." Her voice sounded strange. Distant. "Max. I'm fine."

He exhaled. Nodded. Looked back at the man bleeding on the cobblestones.

"Tell Constantine we're even on the Marrakech incident. And tell her if any of her people come near my assistant again, I'll return more than fingers."

They walked to the car.

Got in.

Drove away.

Lyra didn't look back at the fingers on the stones. She didn't look at Max either.

She was too busy trying to understand why her brain had seized on three words and wouldn't let them go.

*She's mine.*

*She's mine.*

*She's mine.*

---

### THE AFTERMATH

They drove in silence for twenty minutes.

Lyra's hands were shaking. She kept them pressed against her thighs, hoping Max wouldn't notice. He was staring straight ahead, grip white-knuckled on the steering wheel — he'd taken over from the driver, dismissing him with a look that didn't invite argument.

Finally, she spoke.

"Where did you learn to do that?"

"Do what?"

"You know what."

His jaw worked. "The same place I learned everything else. Before I decided to be something different."

"The operation Constantine mentioned. The weapon."

Silence. Then:

"It was called PROMETHEUS." His voice was careful, measured, like he was walking through a minefield. "It wasn't really a weapon. It was... a tool. For perceiving things. For seeing possibilities that normal minds can't process."

"What kind of possibilities?"

"The kind that break you." He exhaled. "I'm not ready to talk about it. Not yet. Maybe not ever. But I need you to know — what I did back there, that's not who I am anymore. That's who I *was*. Who I could have been, if I'd made different choices."

Lyra absorbed this.

"You cut off three of his fingers without blinking."

"Yes."

"You didn't hesitate. Didn't warn him. Just..." She made a gesture she couldn't quite name. "Movement. And then fingers."

"Yes."

"And you said I was yours."

The car swerved slightly. Max corrected, his expression unreadable.

"That was... I didn't mean it the way it sounded."

"What way did you mean it?"

He was quiet for a long time.

"I meant that you're under my protection. That there are lines people don't get to cross. That I've spent years building a life where violence isn't necessary, where kindness works, where the network holds — and anyone who threatens that life, who threatens the people in it..." His voice dropped. "They learn why I stopped being the person I used to be. They learn it *fast*."

Lyra should have been afraid.

She should have been disturbed, horrified, questioning every moment she'd spent in that office, every conversation they'd had, every time she'd thought she understood who he was.

Instead, she was thinking about the way gravity had seemed to *agree* with him in that moment. The way reality itself had bent around his certainty. The way three words had landed in her chest and refused to leave.

*She's mine.*

"Okay," she said finally.

Max blinked. "Okay?"

"Okay. I understand. You used to be dangerous. You chose to be something else. But the dangerous thing is still there, underneath, and when someone threatened..." She paused, choosing her words carefully. "When someone crossed a line, you stopped choosing."

"Yes." The word was barely a whisper.

"That's not who you are. But it's part of what you're capable of."

"Yes."

Lyra nodded slowly.

"Thank you for protecting me."

Max turned to stare at her. The car drifted toward the shoulder before he corrected again.

"That's... that's not the response most people have."

"I know." She looked out the window, watching the city reappear around them. "I'm not most people."

A long silence.

Then: "No. You're really not."

---

### EPILOGUE: 2 AM

Lyra couldn't sleep.

She lay in her apartment — the cheap studio she'd rented when she took this job, the place that had started to feel less like *hers* and more like *the place she slept between moments at the office* — and stared at the ceiling.

Her wrist still tingled where the man had grabbed it.

Her hands were still shaking.

And she couldn't stop hearing it.

*"She's mine."*

Not *"she works for me."*

Not *"she's under my protection."*

*"She's mine."*

The tone of it. The absolute certainty. Like he wasn't making a claim — he was describing reality. Like the universe had already decided she belonged to him, and he was just making sure everyone else knew it.

She should be terrified.

A man she worked for had just demonstrated, without warning or hesitation, that he was capable of surgical violence. That beneath the kindness and the favors and the network of small graces, there was something else. Something that had been trained. Honed. Weaponized.

She should be updating her resume. Looking for other jobs. Getting as far away from Max and his filing cabinet and his sister and his mysterious operation as humanly possible.

Instead, she was lying in the dark, trembling, feeling something she didn't have words for.

Because some part of her — some part she wasn't ready to look at yet — had *liked* it.

Not the violence. Not the blood. But the certainty. The totality. The way he'd stepped between her and harm without a single moment of hesitation.

The way he'd claimed her.

In front of witnesses.

With blood.

And now she was lying in the dark at 2 AM, and her hands wouldn't stop shaking, and she didn't know if it was fear or something else entirely.

*She's mine.*

The words echoed in the dark.

And somewhere deep in her chest, something answered.

*Yes. Okay. Yes.*
