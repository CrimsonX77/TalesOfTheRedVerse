# THE WORLD DOMINATION AGENCY LTD™
## Episode One: "Help Wanted"

---

### COLD OPEN

The rain had opinions about Lyra Chen's life choices.

Specifically, it seemed to think she deserved to be drenched. Thoroughly. Punitively. As if the universe had looked at her morning — the termination email sent at 6:47 AM, the eviction notice slid under her door by 8:00, the coffee shop that got her order wrong and then charged her twice — and decided, *you know what this situation needs? Weather.*

She ducked into the alley because it was a shortcut. Because her shoes were already ruined. Because at this point, what was the worst that could happen? Mugging? At least that would be *interesting.* At least that would be a story. "I got fired, evicted, AND mugged, all before noon" had a certain narrative completeness that "I got fired and evicted and then just... walked home wet" lacked entirely.

The alley smelled like ambition that had given up. Old restaurant grease and older regrets. Puddles that reflected nothing worth seeing.

And then—

The sign.

Hand-painted. Wooden. Hanging from a single chain at a fifteen-degree angle that suggested either artistic intent or structural neglect. The lettering was surprisingly elegant for something clearly produced in someone's garage.

**THE WORLD DOMINATION AGENCY LTD™**
*Now Hiring*
*Inquire Within*
*(Serious Applicants Only)*

Lyra stopped walking.

She read it again.

She laughed — this awful, wet, exhausted sound that came from somewhere below her lungs. The first real laugh in... god, how long? Weeks? Since before Marcus. Since before the "restructuring." Since before she'd stopped being someone things happened *for* and started being someone things happened *to.*

"World Domination," she said aloud, to no one. "Sure. Why not."

The door opened.

---

### ACT ONE: THE INTERVIEW

He was not what she expected.

To be fair, she hadn't expected anything. If pressed, she might have imagined some variety of basement-dwelling conspiracy theorist — pallid, intense, probably with manifestos. Or maybe a performance artist, playing elaborate pranks on a gentrifying neighborhood.

What she got was: six feet of chaotic energy in a slightly-too-large suit jacket, hair that had clearly lost a battle with both gravity and product, and the kind of smile that suggested he'd just remembered a really good joke but was saving it for later.

"Oh!" He seemed genuinely surprised to see her. "A applicant! An actual — hold on, let me just—" He patted his pockets frantically, producing a crumpled piece of paper. "I have questions prepared. Very professional. Very — where did I put the—"

"I'm not actually applying," Lyra heard herself say. "I just saw the sign and..."

"And felt drawn to it?" He looked up, eyes bright. "That's exactly what's supposed to happen! I spent a very long time on the font. The font is *crucial*. Studies show that serif fonts convey authority but sans-serif conveys modernity, so I went with a hybrid approach that I'm calling 'serif-ish' and I think it really—"

"I laughed at it," she interrupted. "I laughed at your sign."

A normal person would have been offended. He beamed.

"*Perfect.* That's the first test. Everyone who takes themselves too seriously misses it entirely. They walk right past. But the ones who laugh?" He stepped back, gesturing her inside with a flourish. "The ones who laugh are the ones who *understand* that the absurd is just the profound wearing a funny hat."

Lyra did not go inside.

But she also didn't leave.

"I'm soaking wet," she said, as if that were a relevant objection.

"I have towels! Well. Towel. Singular. It's got the logo on it though — I had a batch made, there was a minimum order, I have seven hundred towels in a storage unit in Queens. Long story. Excellent towels though. Very absorbent. Would you like to see the office?"

"There's an office?"

He looked almost hurt. "Of course there's an office. What kind of World Domination Agency operates without an *office?*"

---

The office was one room.

This was being generous. It was one room in the same way that a closet was technically a room, or a particularly ambitious refrigerator box could be considered a studio apartment. The walls were exposed brick — not the trendy kind, the "this building is one health inspection away from condemnation" kind. The ceiling featured a water stain that, if you squinted, looked remarkably like the European Union.

There was one desk. One chair. One filing cabinet that appeared to be held together by optimism and a single strip of duct tape.

And on the wall, in a frame that was nicer than anything else in the room, hung a motivational poster. A soaring eagle against a sunset. The caption read: **CONQUER YOUR FEARS.**

Someone had added, in careful Sharpie: *(and also Belgium)*

"Belgium?" Lyra asked.

"Low-hanging fruit," he said, completely serious. "Strategically positioned. Excellent chocolate infrastructure. You have to start somewhere."

She turned to look at him — really look, for the first time. Mid-thirties, maybe. The suit was expensive but didn't fit quite right, like he'd bought it for an occasion that never happened. His eyes were the problem. They were too *awake*. Too present. Like he was seeing six things she couldn't.

"I'm Maximilian St. Claire-Pemberton the Third," he said, extending a hand. "But everyone calls me Max. Well — my mother calls me Maximilian when she's disappointed, which is often, and my Uncle Harold calls me 'the family embarrassment,' but that's really more of a title than a name, don't you think?"

Lyra shook his hand. It was warm and dry and his grip was surprisingly firm.

"Lyra Chen."

"Lyra." He said it like he was tasting the word. "From the constellation. The lyre of Orpheus. Did you know that Orpheus played so beautifully that he could charm stones and rivers? The gods wept when he played. And then he went to the underworld to retrieve his dead wife and almost succeeded except he looked back at the last moment and lost her forever." He paused. "Sorry. That got dark. I do that sometimes. The point is — it's a lovely name."

"Thanks. I think."

"So!" He clapped his hands together, spinning toward the desk with the energy of a man who'd had either too much coffee or not nearly enough. "The position. Chief Administrative Officer of Global Operations."

"That's... a lot of words."

"It's receptionist," he admitted. "But I believe in aspirational titling. How can we expect to dominate the world if we can't even dominate our job descriptions?"

Lyra looked around the room again. At the water stain. At the filing cabinet. At the stack of papers on the desk that appeared to be — yes, that was definitely a five-year plan, color-coded with tab dividers, titled *OPERATION: EVERYTHING* in bold letters.

"What exactly," she said slowly, "does a World Domination Agency *do?*"

Max's smile flickered — not fading, but *shifting*. For just a second, something else was there. Something quieter. Older.

"We solve problems," he said. "Problems that other people don't even know are problems yet. And then, eventually, when we've solved enough of them, when enough people owe us enough favors, when we've become so *essential* that no one can imagine a world without us..." He shrugged. "Domination isn't about conquest, Lyra. It's about becoming *necessary.*"

"That's..." She searched for the word. "Surprisingly coherent?"

"I have my moments." The manic energy returned, the insight buried again under enthusiasm. "Now! The compensation package. I can offer you thirty-seven thousand dollars a year, full dental—"

"Thirty-seven *thousand?*"

"Is that not enough? The internet said that was competitive for administrative positions in this zip code. I can do forty. Forty-two? I had a very good month with cryptocurrency in 2021 and I've been coasting on it ever since, financially speaking. Not professionally. Professionally I'm an embarrassment. Ask anyone."

Lyra's brain was still stuck on the number. Her last job — the one she'd been fired from this morning via a 6:47 AM email — had paid twenty-nine thousand and they'd acted like they were doing her a favor.

"You're offering me forty-two thousand dollars," she said, "to be the receptionist for a fake company in a condemned building."

"First of all, the building isn't condemned. It's 'under review.' There's a difference. Second of all—" He tilted his head, studying her. "What makes you think it's fake?"

"The name. The sign. The..." She gestured at everything. "All of this."

"Ah." He nodded sagely. "The classic mistake. You think that because something is absurd, it can't be real. But consider, Lyra Chen — isn't the most absurd thing in the world the idea that the current systems make *sense?* That the people in charge got there through competence rather than accident? That any of this—" he waved at the general concept of society "—was *designed?*"

She didn't have an answer for that.

"The world is dominated by people who stumbled into power and then convinced everyone it was inevitable," Max continued. "I'm simply proposing to stumble *intentionally.* To trip with *purpose.* To face-plant directly into the levers of control with a comprehensive benefits package and a clearly articulated mission statement." He smiled. "Is that really so crazy?"

"Yes," Lyra said. "Absolutely yes."

"Great! You're hired. Can you start Monday?"

---

### ACT TWO: THE FIRST DAY

She didn't say yes.

She also didn't say no.

What she said was "I need to think about it," and then she went home to the apartment she was being evicted from and sat on the floor of her bedroom surrounded by boxes she couldn't afford movers for and thought about Orpheus, who went to hell for love and almost made it out.

The rain stopped around midnight. By 2 AM, she was laughing again — that same awful, wet sound from the alley. By 3 AM, she was crying. By 4 AM, she was filling out the application she'd found crumpled in her pocket, which Max had apparently slipped in during the handshake.

The form asked three questions:

1. *What is your greatest weakness?*
2. *If you could dominate one thing (other than the world), what would it be?*
3. *Describe a time when you believed in something that didn't make sense.*

She wrote:

1. *I look back.*
2. *My own fear.*
3. *Right now.*

---

Monday arrived like a subpoena.

The office looked different in the morning light — or rather, it looked *exactly the same*, which was somehow different when you were sober and rested and had technically agreed to work here. The water stain still mapped Europe. The filing cabinet still defied physics. The Belgian conquest remained scheduled.

But there was a second desk now.

Small. Tucked into the corner. A nameplate that read: **LYRA CHEN — CHIEF ADMINISTRATIVE OFFICER OF GLOBAL OPERATIONS.**

And a towel, neatly folded. White. Embroidered logo: a tiny globe with a crown on it. Underneath, in elegant script: *WDA Ltd™ — Reach for the Stars, Settle for Belgium.*

"You came!" Max emerged from what she had assumed was a closet but was apparently a bathroom. He was wearing the same suit. Or an identical one. His hair had evolved since Friday, forming new and interesting shapes. "I wasn't sure you would. I prepared remarks for both scenarios. Would you like to hear the speech I wrote in case you didn't show up? It was quite moving. I practiced crying."

"I came," Lyra said, because she didn't know what else to say. "I'm here."

"Yes! Excellent! Okay—" He produced a clipboard. "First order of business. We have a client meeting at noon."

"A *client* meeting?"

"Mrs. Henderson. From 4B. Her cat has been stolen by what she describes as 'the shadow government' but what I suspect is actually her neighbor, Mr. Polaski from 4A, who has complained about said cat for several years and recently purchased a suspiciously large number of lint rollers." He paused. "This is what we do. We start small. We solve Mrs. Henderson's cat problem. And then we solve a slightly larger problem. And then a slightly larger one. And then—"

"Belgium?"

"You're catching on!" He seemed genuinely delighted. "But first — coffee. There's a machine in the bathroom. Don't ask why it's in the bathroom. The answer involves a lease agreement and a very specific interpretation of the phrase 'kitchenette-adjacent.' Do you take cream? Sugar? I have seven hundred packets of both, also from the towel situation."

Lyra sat down at her desk. Her desk. In the headquarters of The World Domination Agency LTD™.

The chair squeaked.

"This is insane," she said.

"Yes," Max agreed, handing her a mug with the same logo as the towel. "But so is everything else. At least here we have a plan."

---

### ACT TWO, PART TWO: THE PROBLEM

Mrs. Henderson was seventy-three years old and absolutely certain that her cat, Mr. Whiskers, had been abducted by agents of a clandestine organization she referred to only as "The Council."

"They've been watching me," she said, clutching a cup of tea that Max had somehow produced from the bathroom-kitchenette. "Ever since I complained about the recycling schedule. You don't *complain* about The Council's recycling schedule, young man. That's how they get you."

Lyra watched Max's face for any sign of mockery. Any hint of condescension.

There was none.

"Mrs. Henderson," he said, leaning forward with complete seriousness, "can you describe the last time you saw Mr. Whiskers?"

"Tuesday. He was on the fire escape. I went to get his treats — the salmon ones, not the chicken, he has *standards* — and when I came back, he was gone. But here's the thing—" She lowered her voice. "The window was still locked. From the *inside.*"

"A locked-room disappearance." Max nodded gravely. "Classic Council methodology."

"*Max,*" Lyra hissed.

He held up a finger. *Wait.*

"Mrs. Henderson, I'm going to ask you something, and I need you to answer honestly. It may seem unrelated. Do you trust me?"

The old woman studied him for a long moment. Then, slowly, she nodded.

"Your neighbor. Mr. Polaski. Has he recently acquired any new... equipment? Ladders, perhaps? Or—"

"A *drone.*" Mrs. Henderson's eyes went wide. "He bought a drone last month. Said it was for 'photography.' But I've seen him flying it near my window. Near my *fire escape.*"

Max sat back, steepling his fingers.

"The Council didn't take your cat, Mrs. Henderson. But someone did. And I think I know how we're going to get him back." He glanced at Lyra. "How do you feel about light trespassing?"

"I feel like that's illegal?"

"Only if you get caught. And we—" He gestured between them, grinning. "—are going to be very, *very* careful."

---

### ACT TWO, PART THREE: THE SHIFT

It was, objectively, ridiculous.

They were staking out an apartment building. From the roof. Because Max had somehow acquired blueprints ("Don't ask") and determined that Mr. Polaski's apartment had an HVAC vulnerability that would allow them to hear inside if they positioned themselves correctly ("Really don't ask").

"This is breaking and entering," Lyra whispered.

"We haven't entered anything. We're just... aggressively loitering." Max was crouched near an air vent, holding what appeared to be a modified stethoscope. "And anyway, if we're right about the cat, we're not breaking laws — we're *correcting* them. Mr. Polaski committed theft. We're simply gathering evidence for Mrs. Henderson to present to the appropriate authorities."

"And if we're wrong?"

"Then we've had a lovely evening on a rooftop and learned valuable lessons about making assumptions." He pressed the stethoscope to the vent. "Now shush. I'm trying to hear if—"

He went still.

Not the usual kinetic stillness of Max pausing between thoughts. This was different. Every muscle in his body seemed to lock into place. His eyes, which had been dancing with mischief, went flat. Focused. *Sharp.*

"There's someone else in there," he said quietly. "Not Polaski. Someone—"

The rooftop access door slammed open.

Three men. Large. Professional-looking. The kind of professional that wore suits to funerals they'd caused.

"Mr. St. Claire-Pemberton." The one in front smiled. It was the smile of a shark who'd learned the expression from a PowerPoint presentation. "Our employer would like a word."

Lyra's heart stopped. This was it. This was where the joke ended and reality — cold, hard, terrifying reality — came crashing down. They were going to die on a rooftop because of a *cat* and she'd only been employed for eight hours and—

"Your employer." Max's voice was different. Lower. Calm in a way that made the air feel heavy. "Would that be Mr. Constantine? Or has he finally delegated his intimidation to someone with actual taste in tailoring?"

The lead man's smile flickered.

"You know, I've been wondering when he'd notice me." Max stood slowly. Unfolded. He seemed taller somehow. The ill-fitting suit suddenly looked intentional, like a costume he could shed at any moment. "Setting up shop in his territory. Solving his problems before he could charge for them. Very rude of me, really."

"Mr. Constantine doesn't appreciate competition."

"Then Mr. Constantine should try being more competent." Max took a step forward. The three men were all taller than him, broader, clearly armed. He looked like he was walking into a board meeting. "Tell your employer that The World Domination Agency is not a threat. We're an *inevitability.* And inevitabilities don't negotiate."

"We're not here to negotiate."

"No," Max agreed. "You're here to deliver a message. Well—" He smiled, and it wasn't the manic grin from the office. It was something older. Colder. The kind of smile that had seen things and decided to smile anyway. "Message received. Now deliver mine: The next time Constantine sends people to threaten me, I'll send them back in a state that requires assembly."

Silence.

The lead man's hand twitched toward his jacket.

Max didn't move. Didn't blink. Just stood there, a lanky silhouette against the city lights, and *waited.*

The moment stretched. Pulled. Threatened to snap.

And then—

The men left.

Just... left. Filed back through the door. Footsteps retreating down the stairs. The rooftop empty except for them and the whirring of distant traffic.

Lyra realized she'd stopped breathing.

"Max. What the *fuck* was that?"

He turned back to her, and the transition was instant — the cold presence melting back into chaotic warmth, the sharp edges softening into something almost boyish.

"That," he said, "was a complication. We should probably talk about it. But first—" He crouched back by the vent, stethoscope in hand. "I definitely heard a cat. And I refuse to let Mrs. Henderson down because some second-rate crime lord has boundary issues."

"Second-rate *crime lord?*"

"Constantine. Runs about thirty percent of the grey-market operations in this district. Protection rackets, mostly. Money laundering. Very boring, very predictable, very much in the way of what I'm trying to build." He adjusted the stethoscope. "I'll explain everything. I promise. But right now, I need you to trust me for about six more minutes."

Lyra stared at him.

The man who'd rambled about fonts and serif-ish lettering. Who had seven hundred towels in a storage unit. Who'd offered her dental and sovereignty in the same sentence.

The man who'd just stared down three armed thugs like they were mild inconveniences.

"Six minutes," she said.

He grinned — the real one, the manic one, the one she was beginning to realize might be the mask instead of the face underneath.

"You're going to be *great* at this job."

---

### ACT THREE: THE CHOICE

They saved the cat.

It was, in retrospect, almost anticlimactic. Max identified the HVAC connection to Polaski's apartment, they recorded approximately four minutes of the man talking to Mr. Whiskers in baby voice ("*Who's daddy's little kidnapped angel? Who's going to help daddy get revenge on the mean lady who reported him to the housing board?*"), and the next morning, Mrs. Henderson had her cat back and Mr. Polaski had a visit from animal control.

Case closed. Client satisfied. The World Domination Agency's first official success.

But Lyra couldn't stop thinking about the rooftop.

"You're not just a guy with a dream and a filing cabinet," she said, standing in the office the next morning. Her desk. Her nameplate. Her towel, which she had to admit was extremely absorbent. "Who are you? Really?"

Max was quiet for a long moment. He'd made coffee again — bathroom machine, questionable provenance — and now he stood by the window, looking out at the alley. The light caught the angles of his face differently here. Made him look tired. Made him look real.

"I used to be someone else," he said finally. "Someone with resources. Connections. A family name that opened doors." He took a sip of coffee. "And then I asked the wrong questions about the right people, and I became someone who had to disappear."

"So this—" she gestured at the office, the sign, the binder labeled OPERATION: EVERYTHING "—is what? A disguise?"

"It's a *starting point.*" He turned to face her. "I can't fight the systems that broke me, Lyra. Not head-on. Not with what I have left. But I can build something new. Something small at first. Something that looks ridiculous until suddenly it doesn't. Something that solves problems for people like Mrs. Henderson until one day, I've solved enough problems that the people who made me disappear have to take me seriously."

"And then?"

"And then we negotiate." His eyes glittered. "From a position of *strength.*"

Lyra thought about the email at 6:47 AM. The eviction notice. The rain that had opinions about her life choices. She thought about walking through an alley and seeing a sign and laughing for the first time in weeks.

She thought about Orpheus, who went to hell and almost made it out.

"The job you're offering me," she said slowly. "It's not really receptionist work, is it?"

"It's whatever you want it to be." Max set down his coffee. "I won't lie to you, Lyra. This is dangerous. The people who want me gone are powerful, and they'll notice you eventually. You can walk away right now. I'll give you severance — three months, full pay. Enough to find something normal. Something safe."

She looked around the office. The water stain. The filing cabinet. The motivational poster with its Sharpied amendment.

*CONQUER YOUR FEARS (and also Belgium)*

"Normal hasn't worked out great for me," she said. "And safe is boring."

Max's smile broke across his face like sunrise.

"Welcome to The World Domination Agency, Chief Administrative Officer Chen." He extended his hand. "Let's go conquer something."

She shook it.

The chair squeaked.

And somewhere in the distance, entirely unrelated to anything, a cat meowed.

---

### TAG

Later — much later — Lyra would try to remember the exact moment she'd decided to stay.

Was it the sign? The laugh? The speech about stumbling with purpose?

Was it watching him transform on that rooftop, the goofball mask slipping to reveal something precise and dangerous underneath?

Was it the cat? The coffee? The towels?

In the end, she decided it didn't matter. Some things couldn't be pinned to a single moment. Some things were just *true*, and you spent your whole life walking toward them without knowing it.

She'd been lost. Broken. Drowning in a world that didn't make sense.

And then she'd found a door with a crooked sign, and a man with a plan, and a chair that squeaked.

And nothing made sense here either.

But at least — *finally* — she had company.
