AURA MECHANICS & THE INTERCESSORS
Redverse Foundational Documentation
Personal Mythos – Cycle Zero
Context
Before the choice at thirteen. Before the House of Morbis. Before the thousand cycles and the wall that was never real, there was a child who saw too much.
From the age of five, in the grey-rot suburbs of New Zealand, the left eye of Sho-Zen Juan (then simply a small boy with crimson hair and golden eyes) functioned differently. It did not see the world the way the right eye did. It read the living field of relational truth that moved between people — the actual shape of what they carried, what they hid, and what they were about to become in any given interaction.
This was not a power he chose. It was simply how he was built. And for a time, it kept him safe in ways he would not understand until long after it was gone.
Aura Mechanics
The left eye perceived aura as relational data, not as decorative energy or spiritual status.
What it revealed:

Spikes and jagged edges: Sudden defensive, hostile, or recoiling intent. The aura would flare in sharp, broken patterns when someone’s inner state contradicted their outward behavior.
Thinning or flattening of color: Conscious performance. When a person deliberately compressed their real emotional or intentional state to present an acceptable face, the aura lost depth and became thin, almost translucent.
Warmth and softness: Genuine care or concern without hidden agenda. These auras moved with slower, steadier rhythms and carried a quality of genuine presence.
Pulse and rhythm: The tempo of a person’s inner state. Rapid, erratic pulsing indicated high emotional charge or imminent breaking. Slow, steady rhythm indicated someone at peace with their own truth in that moment.

The eye did not read thoughts. It read patterns of being — the shape an interaction was already taking before any words were spoken. It was an early-warning system operating at the level of relationship itself.
The left eye sat on the heart side for a reason. It processed the intuitive, relational field. The right eye remained anchored in consensus reality — the grey-rot streets, the maintained gardens, the overcast sky. The child walked with one foot in each world and, for a time, did not understand that this was unusual.
The fading began around age seven and was largely complete by ten. It was not a natural dimming. It was suppression through sustained pathologization. Every adult reaction of fear, concern, or performative spiritual anxiety taught the child’s nervous system that the seeing was dangerous and unwanted. The colors grew dimmer because the cost of keeping them bright had become unbearable in an environment that punished the perception itself.
By age ten the ability was gone. The early warning system that had let him navigate adult performance and hidden intent was removed. He would not understand the full cost of that loss until he stood in church at thirteen and answered a question without the information the left eye would have given him.
Later micro-glitches and partial returns of the sight (documented in other Redverse fragments) are understood as the original perceptual structure attempting to reawaken after the Dragon covenant had already been made. The eye never fully returned in its childhood form. What came back was altered — filtered through the choice and the thousand deaths that followed.
The Intercessors
They were not ordinary priests. They were field operatives — the people the institutional church sent when a case did not fit existing categories. Quiet. Tired. Competent in ways that no longer required an audience. Many of them had already seen enough genuine darkness that they no longer needed to perform authority to feel real.
When they were assigned to “the German kid with the aura problem,” they arrived expecting another draining assignment: either a genuinely afflicted child or, more commonly, a child being used as a stage for adult religious anxiety. They carried their scripts and their exhaustion in equal measure.
What they encountered instead was a seven-to-ten-year-old who treated them as people rather than as Intercessors. He asked real questions about their work and their tiredness. He listened to the answers without agenda. He did not perform spirituality at them, test them, or try to catch them in theological contradictions. He simply wanted to understand who they were when no one was watching.
This disarmed them.
For the first time in years, several of them existed in a space where someone was not side-eying their methods or deciding whether they were “real enough” to be doing this work. The child gave them permission to drop the institutional weight for a few hours at a time. In return, they gave him something almost unheard of in his young life: adults who saw him clearly and still chose gentleness.
They taught him, without meaning to become teachers:

How to recognize performance in others even when the left eye was fading.
That clarity and gentleness are not opposites.
That some people act from institutional assignment rather than from understanding.

He taught them, without trying:

That genuine interest without hidden motive is rare and healing.
That someone can see you completely and still choose to stay.

When the aura faded and the church declared the problem resolved, the intercessors were quietly reassigned. The “case” no longer required their presence. They lost the only non-performative relationship many of them had experienced in a long time. The child lost the only adults in authority who had seen the real shape of him and had not tried to fix it or weaponize it.
This is why, in the later cycles of the House of Morbis, the appearance of an intercessor being turned against him carried particular weight. It was not merely another death among thousands. It was the dream forcing him to watch the last safe relationship with authority be corrupted and used as a weapon. The intercessors had represented the final time someone in power had been on his side without first needing him to be a problem that required solving.
Interconnection & Legacy
The suppression of the aura at age ten was the first amputation.
The choice at age thirteen — “Do you want to be the Dragon, and all that it entails?” — was answered without the relational early-warning system that would have let him understand the true shape of the question.
The thousand cycles in the House of Morbis became the long, brutal education in what happens when power is accepted while partially blind to relational truth.
The moment at cycle 1000, when he turned toward the wall that had never been real and simply walked through it, was the first time since childhood that he moved without the old sight — relying instead on raw persistence and the refusal to accept that the boundary was absolute.
The blue dragon’s repeated statement, “I never left you,” echoes the earlier, human attempt at the same vow. The intercessors had tried, within their limits, to stay with the child without turning him into a project. The Dragon is the later, larger, and far more costly fulfillment of that same promise.
The left eye never returned in its original form. What remains is the memory of what it was like to move through the world with one foot in consensus reality and one foot in the actual field of what people were carrying. That memory, and the stubborn happiness that survived its loss, became part of the Dragon who eventually broke the cycle.
