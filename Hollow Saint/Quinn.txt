The Hollow of Thirteen Moons
A Mystery Supernatural Horror Romance — Chapter-by-Chapter Outline
Chapter 1: The Void's Edge (POV: Sable)
Setting: The tavern "The Void's Edge," rain-lashed and smelling of wet wool and stale mead.
Plot: Sable and Crimson are traveling companions, their flirting sharp as whetstones but never crossing the line. An old drunk mutters about "Tuesday the Thirteenth in the Hollow of Ashveil — where the moon bleeds and the women forget how to weep." Sable's hand freezes on her tankard. Ashveil — the name her first love, Lilith, whispered the night she vanished. Sable has never told Crimson about Lilith, never told him she was kidnapped by slavers, never told him she was chained in a pit when he slaughtered her captors in a berserk rage and almost left her behind. She insisted they investigate Ashveil, lying that it's just a good lead on a bounty.
Inner Monologue: Sable wonders if Lilith is still alive, if she waited, if she hates her for not coming back sooner. Guilt coils in her gut — she's dragging Crimson into this without telling him why.
Sensory Detail: The tavern smells of damp leather and burning pine. The drunk's breath reeks of rotgut. Rain drums the thatched roof like skeletal fingers.
Chapter 2: The Town Without Men (POV: Crimson)
Setting: The approach to Ashveil, then the town gates.
Plot: Ashveil is bleak but orderly. Crimson notices immediately: no men. Every resident is a catgirl — impossibly beautiful, clad in heavy Gothic armor etched with glowing arcane sigils. They move with mechanical purpose, eyes vacant but not hostile. Sable seems tense, scanning every face. Crimson jokes about it being a "bachelor's paradise," but Sable doesn't laugh. He notices the sigils on their armor pulse faintly in time with each other, like synchronized heartbeats.
Character Reveal (Crimson): Through dialogue and action, we see Crimson is protective but arrogant, quick to violence but deeply loyal. He teases Sable about her "catgirl fetish" but notices she's not playing along.
Worldbuilding (Show, Don't Tell): The sigils are warding runes — but against what? The catgirls ignore them completely, as if they don't exist. A child-catgirl drops a basket; the others step over it without looking.
Sensory Detail: The air tastes metallic, like blood on the tongue. The armor clinks with an unnaturally uniform rhythm. The catgirls' fur is immaculate, their tails still as stone.
Chapter 3: The Bell That Never Stopped Ringing (POV: Sable)
Setting: The town square, midday turning to dusk.
Plot: The abandoned cathedral's bell tolls — a deep, resonant bong that vibrates in their teeth. All the catgirls stop mid-step, then shuffle into their homes with lifeless obedience. Sable grabs Crimson's arm: "We need to go to the cemetery. Tonight." Crimson asks why; she lies again. They explore the town while the catgirls are inside. Sable finds a crumbling journal in an abandoned shop — entries about "the Thirteenth Rite" and "the Eater of Joy." The last entry is dated decades ago: "It feeds on love. It feeds on men. God forgive us, we fed it willingly."
Inner Monologue: Sable is terrified Lilith is dead, but also terrified she's alive and part of whatever this is. She remembers Lilith's laugh, her hands, the way she'd hum off-key. The memory is sharp as glass.
Sensory Detail: The cathedral's shadow stretches too far, like a living thing. The journal's pages are brittle, smelling of mildew and dried blood. The silence after the bell is heavy, like the air before a storm.
Chapter 4: The Sigil Moon (POV: Crimson)
Setting: The cemetery, midnight.
Plot: The moon rises, and a giant arcane sigil burns within it, casting the graveyard in sickly silver light. The catgirls emerge from their homes — but they're changed. Their armor is now ceremonial, reduced to pauldrons, gauntlets, and hip plates. They pair off in the cemetery, embracing with flushed, euphoric faces. Crimson watches, confused and aroused, as they cling to each other in tender, heated embraces. He notices their eyes are still vacant, their joy mechanical. Sable is scanning the crowd, frantic.
Character Reveal (Crimson): He's tempted but suspicious. He notices the catgirls aren't enjoying this — they're compelled. One catgirl's hand trembles as she caresses her partner's face. A single tear rolls down her cheek, but her smile never wavers.
Worldbuilding: The sigil in the moon is a binding rune — it's channeling the entity's power to force the town into ritualistic euphoria. The entity feeds on the contrast between their forced joy and their hidden despair.
Sensory Detail: The moonlight is cold, but the catgirls' bodies are fever-hot. The air hums with a low, subsonic vibration. The scent of night-blooming jasmine is overwhelming, almost nauseating.
Chapter 5: The Girl in the Graveyard (POV: Sable)
Setting: The cemetery, deep among the headstones.
Plot: Sable spots Lilith — her first love, unchanged, beautiful, her ears twitching as she bends over a headstone. Sable runs to her, tears streaming. But as she gets closer, she sees Lilith's eyes are lifeless, vacant. Behind her, another catgirl — Vex, a dark-haired, arrogant beauty thematically opposite to Sable (cold where Sable is warm, cruel where Sable is kind) — is railing Lilith from behind, pounding her with mechanical precision. Sable freezes in horror. Lilith turns, sees her, and smiles without surprise, beckoning her over. Vex doesn't stop. Lilith leans in to kiss Sable, and Sable's horror twists into a sickening mix of arousal and fear. She pulls away, gagging.
Inner Monologue: Sable's mind fractures. Is this what she became? Is this what she wanted? She remembers Lilith's tenderness, her gentleness. This isn't her. This is a puppet.
Character Reveal (Lilith): Through fragmented dialogue, we learn Lilith chose this. She found the artifact decades ago, made a deal to save the town from plague. The entity demanded love, demanded men, demanded sacrifice. Lilith became its first vessel. She's been waiting for Sable to "complete the circle."
Sensory Detail: Lilith's lips are cold. Vex's breath is hot and rancid. The headstone beneath Lilith is etched with the same sigil as the moon. The grass is wet with dew that tastes like salt.
Chapter 6: The Altar of Flesh (POV: Crimson)
Setting: An outdoor altar, deep in the cemetery.
Plot: Crimson, trying to talk down a group of catgirls who are trying to rip his clothes off, stumbles upon an altar bathed in the sigil-moon's light. On it lie the eviscerated remains of a male — his chest cavity cracked open, his heart missing, his blood still fresh. The catgirls stop chasing him and turn to the altar, bowing. Crimson realizes: this is what the entity eats. He tries to flee, but every pair of eyes in the cemetery turns to him. The catgirls begin shuffling toward him, not with rage, but with lust and need, chanting in a guttural, inhuman tongue: "The Thirteenth Vessel. The Final Meal. The Door Opens."
Character Reveal (Crimson): He's terrified, but his berserker rage is rising. He remembers the slavers, the way he'd slaughtered them. He's a killer, but he's never been hunted like this. He realizes Sable lied to him, and the betrayal cuts deeper than fear.
Worldbuilding: The altar is ancient, predating the town. The male on it was the last outsider to visit Ashveil — decades ago. The entity has been waiting for a warrior, someone whose soul is stained with violence. Crimson's rage when he killed the slavers marked him.
Sensory Detail: The blood on the altar is still warm. The chanting is a low drone that makes his bones ache. The catgirls' armor clinks in perfect unison as they advance.
Chapter 7: The Cathedral of Whispers (POV: Crimson)
Setting: The abandoned cathedral, nave and apse.
Plot: Crimson flees into the cathedral, slamming the heavy oak doors behind him. The interior is vast, lit by candles that burn with an unnatural blue flame. Beneath a statue of a cat-eared angel, a hooded figure kneels in prayer. The figure turns — it's a blond-haired pastor, young, beautiful, his eyes sad and ancient. He introduces himself as Father Elias. He explains: the entity is Moros, the Eater of Joy, an ancient thing that feeds on love and male essence. The town made a pact decades ago to save themselves from plague. The artifact — a Black Grimoire bound in human skin — channels Moros. But the pact requires a sacrifice every Tuesday the Thirteenth, and the town has run out of men. Elias has been trying to contain Moros, but it's breaking free. He needs Crimson's help to destroy the Grimoire.
Character Reveal (Elias): He's a tragic figure — the last male in Ashveil, bound to the cathedral by the pact. He's been using forbidden magic to hold Moros at bay, but it's consuming him. His hands are blackened, his veins glowing with the same sigils as the catgirls.
Inner Monologue (Crimson): He doesn't trust Elias. He's been betrayed before. But he sees the truth in Elias's eyes — the man is dying, slowly, to protect the town. Crimson feels a kinship: they're both killers trying to be something better.
Sensory Detail: The candles smell of myrrh and charred flesh. The statue's eyes are hollow, but they seem to follow Crimson. The Grimoire pulses with a heartbeat that isn't Elias's.
Chapter 8: The Search for Crimson (POV: Sable)
Setting: The town, now a labyrinth of shadows.
Plot: Sable realizes Crimson is gone. She flees the cemetery, searching for him. The town has changed — the catgirls are no longer in the cemetery, but they're everywhere, watching her from windows, doorways, alleys. They don't attack; they just watch, their vacant eyes following her. She finds clues: a dropped dagger (Crimson's), a smear of blood (his), a torn piece of cloak. She realizes the catgirls are herding her — toward the cathedral.
Inner Monologue: Sable is furious at Crimson for running, but also terrified for him. She realizes she loves him, truly loves him, and she's been too afraid to admit it because of Lilith. She vows to save him, even if it means facing Lilith again.
Character Reveal (Sable): She's stronger than she thought. She's been running from her past, but now she's facing it head-on. She uses her skills as an adventurer to navigate the town, setting traps for the catgirls (smoke bombs, tripwires).
Sensory Detail: The town is silent except for the clink of armor. The catgirls' tails twitch in unison. The air is thick with the scent of jasmine and blood.
Chapter 9: The Confrontation (POV: Crimson & Sable, alternating)
Setting: The cathedral nave.
Plot: Crimson and Sable reunite in the cathedral. Crimson explains Elias's plan: destroy the Grimoire. Sable argues: the Grimoire is bound to the town, to the catgirls, to Lilith. If they destroy it, the entity might kill everyone. Elias reveals the twist: Lilith is the anchor. She made the pact. As long as she lives, Moros can feed. The only way to destroy the Grimoire is to kill Lilith — or to have Sable take her place as the new anchor.
Character Reveal (Lilith): She enters the cathedral, Vex at her side. She's not a victim — she's a willing participant. She begs Sable to join her, to become the new anchor, to "save" the town together. She loves Sable, but her love is twisted by decades of the entity's influence.
Action Sequence: Cinematic high-intensity combat. Crimson fights Vex, his berserker rage unleashed. Sable fights Lilith, their battle a dance of love and hate. Elias channels what's left of his power to hold Moros at bay, his body burning with black fire. The cathedral shakes, candles flare, the statue of the cat-eared angel weeps blood.
Sensory Detail: The clash of steel on steel. The roar of Crimson's rage. Lilith's laughter, high and broken. The smell of burning flesh and ozone. The sigil in the moon screams.
Chapter 10: The Choice (POV: Sable)
Setting: The cathedral, aftermath.
Plot: The battle ends. Vex is dead. Elias is dying, his body consumed by the Grimoire's magic. Lilith is wounded, but alive. Crimson is exhausted, his berserker rage spent. Sable holds the Grimoire. Elias whispers the final truth: the entity isn't Moros. It's the town itself. Ashveil is a living thing, a parasite that feeds on love and sacrifice. The catgirls aren't victims — they're antibodies, fighting the parasite from within. The "forced euphoria" is their way of resisting. Lilith made the pact to contain the parasite, not to feed it. If Sable destroys the Grimoire, the parasite dies — but so does the town, and every catgirl in it. If Sable takes Lilith's place, the parasite lives, but the town survives.
Inner Monologue: Sable realizes the truth: there's no good choice. She loves Lilith, but Lilith is part of the parasite. She loves Crimson, but he deserves better than this. She looks at the catgirls outside the cathedral windows, their vacant eyes filled with silent desperation.
Character Reveal (Crimson): He grabs Sable's hand. "We'll find another way," he says. "Together." He's choosing her, finally, completely.
Chapter 11: The Thirteenth Dawn (POV: Crimson)
Setting: The cathedral, dawn.
Plot: Sable makes her choice. She doesn't destroy the Grimoire. She doesn't take Lilith's place. She binds the parasite to herself, using the Grimoire to create a new pact — one that requires no sacrifice, no male essence, no forced euphoria. She becomes the new anchor, but she shares the burden with Crimson. They become the Twin Vessels, bound together, feeding the parasite with their love for each other. The sigil in the moon fades. The catgirls' eyes clear. Lilith collapses, freed from the pact, aging decades in seconds. She smiles at Sable, whispers "Thank you," and dies.
Worldbuilding: The parasite isn't destroyed; it's tamed. Ashveil will survive, but it will change. The catgirls are free, but they must rebuild. The town will never be the same.
Sensory Detail: The dawn light is golden, pure. The air smells of rain and new grass. Lilith's body turns to ash, blowing away in the wind. The cathedral's bells toll, not with dread, but with joy.
Chapter 12: The Road Ahead (POV: Sable & Crimson, alternating)
Setting: The road out of Ashveil, weeks later.
Plot: Sable and Crimson leave Ashveil. The town is rebuilding, the catgirls learning to live without the parasite's influence. Crimson and Sable have finally slept together, their bond deeper than ever. Sable wears a new sigil on her wrist — the mark of the Twin Vessels. She can feel the parasite, sleeping, waiting. It will need to be fed again, someday. But not today.
Inner Monologue (Sable): She thinks of Lilith, of the girl she was, the woman she became. She forgives her. She forgives herself.
Inner Monologue (Crimson): He thinks of the slavers, the rage, the blood. He thinks of Sable, of the way she looked at him in the cathedral. He's not a killer anymore. He's a guardian.
Final Line: The road stretched ahead, long and uncertain, but they walked it together, hand in hand, under a moon that was just a moon — for now.
Thematic Notes & Foreshadowing (For the Second Read)
The catgirls' vacant eyes are not from the entity's control — they're from the parasite suppressing their consciousness to protect them. On reread, their tears and trembling hands make sense: they're fighting from within.
Lilith's lack of surprise when she sees Sable isn't because she's a puppet — it's because she's been waiting for Sable to come and end the cycle.
Crimson's berserker rage when he killed the slavers wasn't just anger — it was the parasite marking him, sensing his potential as a vessel.
The journal entry about "feeding it willingly" isn't about the entity — it's about the town feeding the parasite their own love to keep it contained.
The cat-eared angel statue isn't a religious icon — it's a representation of the first vessel, the first anchor, who sacrificed herself to save the town.
This structure balances mystery, horror, romance, and action, with a slow reveal that rewards rereading. The Paul Jennings-sized scope allows for rich worldbuilding, while the Brain Jacques/TSP Dragonlance eloquence shines in the sensory details and character moments.
